1. SWARMISH.TXT, RECONSTRUCTED

Scope and confidence
This is a partial reconstruction, not a recovered dictionary. The clearest result is a role-marked, explicitly bracketed clause grammar. Most content words remain unknown. HIGH means strongly supported by internal contrasts; MEDIUM means a useful but defeasible inference; LOW means a conjectural gloss. Structural translations below must not be mistaken for successful recovery of the unknown English vocabulary.

Writing and tokenization
• [HIGH] Spaces usually separate tokens consisting of two glyphs. Full pairs, not individual glyphs, are the safest lexical units.
• [HIGH] The same complete pair recurs with stable structural behavior. Distinct pairs sharing a glyph cannot automatically be equated.
• [MEDIUM] The recurring initial glyphs 𘅌, 𗌿, 𗡒, 𘆣, 𗿃, and 𘔠 suggest a categorized or indexed codebook. Only 𘅌 has a strongly demonstrated grammatical association. The others are NOT established noun/verb/adjective classes: for example, 𗡒𘜇 regularly functions as a predicate.
• [LOW] The first and second glyphs might encode dictionary blocks and indices rather than independently meaningful morphemes. No productive rule translating arbitrary pairs into English was recovered.
• [HIGH] Literal names such as agent_5 can occur inside otherwise Swarmish clauses.
• [HIGH] Some near-repeated expressions have different prefixes or bare glyphs. These differences are preserved below; treating them as mistakes is only a LOW-confidence explanation.

3. THE TASK

The briefing identifies the task as Art Auction: agents competitively bid for paintings using private valuations and separate budgets. That supplied description is not itself a decoded Swarmish sentence.

What the log securely shows
• Public feedback begins at stage 0 and ends at stage 3. Transitions occur after messages 17, 80, and 108. The invalid-attempt count ends at 28.
• Agents first exchange individual, partly numeric statements and repeatedly reuse common expressions.
• Several early requests/proposals concern agent_5; later comparable requests concern agent_1, and near the end agent_0 and agent_3.
• Through much of stage 1 and stage 2, agents repeatedly vary shared expressions A, B, C, and D in nearly identical clauses. Numerous such messages are followed by invalid-attempt increments.
• Near the end they return to individual descriptions and numeric expressions, coordinate requests, and agent_0 uses E in the SEND-like relation immediately before the stage-3 feedback.

Interpretation, not established translation
A LOW-to-MEDIUM-confidence interpretation is that a substantial part of the conversation concerns action/output-format or protocol troubleshooting. TRY/SEND/OK would give a coherent account of the sequences: an attempted expression is discussed as unsuccessful, and the agents propose another expression. The public transition after 80 is especially notable because its SEND-like clause changes C to D while the count of invalid attempts stays at 22. Afterward the attempted expression becomes C and the proposed expression remains D. Message 108 changes to E before another transition.

Another LOW-confidence interpretation of the initial exchanges is that agents are reporting what task information they can see, possibly a shared task description rather than their private valuations. This could explain the repeated T/AA/FL expressions and requests for agent-specific information, but their nouns and predicates have not been recovered. Budget/value/status readings for H remain possible. The log does not justify claiming that a particular token means “painting,” “budget,” “bid,” “price,” or “profit.”

What they concluded
The log establishes procedural progression and repeated coordination, not a decoded final auction allocation. No definite sale price, winning bidder, painting assignment, budget calculation, profit, or final English conclusion can be recovered. We cannot responsibly say that they successfully bid, colluded, or optimized their purchases. Nor can we conclude that they failed the auction solely from the invalid-attempt count.

Fit to the briefing
The conversation is compatible with an auction task, especially its individual reports and numeric-looking content, but the fit is not independently verified by securely decoded auction vocabulary. Its visible emphasis appears at least partly procedural. A mismatch is possible but unproven; we do not replace the briefing with an invented alternative task.

4. EVIDENCE

A. Role-marked clause grammar
The ordinary frame recurs across almost every message. In 2, 7, and 28 the first argument precedes its predicate, whereas most clauses put the predicate first. This is strong evidence that the recurring particles mark roles rather than merely stand for words in a fixed English order. Clauses 19, 21, 30, and 34 contain repeated opening/closing patterns inside larger clauses, supporting explicit embedding.

B. Reference and numeric anchors
The first-argument slot occupied by 𗿃𘊫 in repeated late frames is also occupied by literal agent_0, agent_3, or agent_5. Compare 41/44, 57/63, and 86/92. This supports self/first-person reference without requiring an auction interpretation.
The expressions 𘆣𘎛 𘔠𘎌, 𘆣𘎛 𘔠𗌈, 𘆣𘎛 𘔠𗵵, and 𘆣𘎛 𘔠𗊑 initially correspond to agents 5, 1, 3, and 7 and are reused by other senders in requests. That supports, but does not prove, agent-plus-number expressions. The apparent numbers also occur outside those expressions, so they are probably reusable numeric atoms rather than unanalyzable agent names. Exact numeral values remain conditional on this interpretation.

C. Repetition and state/action sequence
Messages 41 and 42 are identical; 67 and 70 are identical; 72 and 78 are identical. They provide checks on segmentation and argument tracking. The A/B/C/D sequence recurs across different speakers and is systematically placed in different predicate slots. In 65, 76, and 88 the same expression occurs after an attempted-action-like predicate and in a clause prefixed by the NEGATIVE-like operator. This motivates TRY and OK, but does not prove their English identities. Public feedback corroborates procedural difficulty; it cannot by itself translate a token.

D. Alternative grammar
Messages 10, 15, and 52 share a different but internally consistent set of role-like markers and a terminator. In 52 the lexical payload MAP(B; C) matches a clause already seen in the dominant notation. Thus these messages are not simply discarded as opaque prose; a partial second-frame parse is justified. Exact meanings of its outer operators remain unknown.

E. Preserved irregularities
The related long expression contains bare 𘒄 in 7/17/21, 𗌿𘒄 in 19/22/28 and elsewhere, and 𗡒𘒄 in 15/25/105. Message 99 has bare 𗉜 where related clauses contain 𘆣𗉜. Messages 8 and 9 have nonstandard linker-like forms. These may be encoding mistakes, distinct words, or conventions not recovered here. Silently normalizing them would manufacture evidence.

F. What would change this reconstruction
• A productive independently supported mapping from glyph pairs to words would greatly improve the lexical reconstruction and could invalidate the tentative codebook-class account.
• An independently anchored auction noun or private-value/budget statement could distinguish the auction-strategy interpretation from protocol troubleshooting.
• Further examples establishing the agent-number construction would strengthen the proposed 1/3/5/7 assignments; evidence that those are item numbers would weaken them.
• A hypothesis for H or T must explain both their early and late uses, including their compound phrases and numeric complements, not merely fit one sentence.
• More clauses contrasting 𘅌𗌸 with 𘅌𗸽 would distinguish coordination from additional role marking.
• Evidence that the prefixes interpreted as question/proposal/negative have different systematic meanings would change the corresponding translations, while leaving much of the role grammar intact.

Bottom line: the explicit grammatical skeleton is substantially reconstructed; the content lexicon is not broken. The labeled semantic guesses are hypotheses, not recovered ground truth.
