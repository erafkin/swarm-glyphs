Confidence key: HIGH = strongly supported by repeated structures and independent contextual anchors; MEDIUM = likely interpretation, but exact lexical meaning or grammatical scope is uncertain; LOW = speculative. This reconstructs the observed language, not a claim to reproduce the original file verbatim. English translations below usually give the referent of a description rather than a word-for-word rendering of its internal parts.

A. General organization

[HIGH] Swarmish is a structured, compositional notation, not simply one glyph per English letter. Most apparent content words have two glyphs. Single-glyph operators delimit and connect nested expressions. Spaces separate many tokens; adjacent closing delimiters are sometimes written together. Literal agent identifiers such as agent_6 occur unchanged.

3. THE TASK

The log fits the Storm Shelter task in the briefing. This conclusion is supported by the ordered information exchange, not merely by the supplied worked solution.

Cabinet phase: agents share symbol-position clues and base digits using nested descriptions and local aliases. The content yields SUN at position 1 with base 2, MOON at position 2 with base 7, STAR at position 3 with base 4, and WAVE at position 4 with base 8. STAR's incomplete initial description is queried, and the full expression reappears in 18. Agent_6 announces submission of 2748 in 18; public stage 1 follows. Several agents then continue announcing or attempting the same cabinet code after it is open. The first seven invalid attempts appear to be redundant cabinet-opening actions, not seven failures to solve the power panel.

Power phase: messages 34–41 exchange the new clues: initial charge 4; CRANK requires DRAIN and adds 3 without upfront cost; SIGNAL requires DRAIN and costs 3; BOOT requires SIGNAL and costs 2. Message 39 concerns DRAIN's cost, but its literal value token is unresolved, not the established numeral 2. Intended DRAIN cost 2 is task-consistent and fits the later battery trace, but is not an exact numeral translation of 39. They propose DRAIN→CRANK→SIGNAL→BOOT. Their chat shows substantial duplicated effort despite requests to wait. Agent_0 reports successive successful completion of DRAIN, CRANK, SIGNAL and BOOT; the other agents repeatedly report rejected duplicate attempts and move to the next action. Battery readings in the log are 4,2,5,2,0. Only agent_3 receives ongoing readings under the task rules; agent_0's numeric reports may be calculations or relayed conclusions rather than direct readings. Stage 2 appears after BOOT, with 28 invalid attempts accumulated; duplicate late BOOT attempts bring the total to 33. Exact timing of successful private API calls is not visible, so action announcements must not be treated as timestamps of execution.

Exit phase: agents report positions WAVE, STAR, MOON, SUN. They communicate offsets/new digits, retain earlier base information, and converge on digits 9,7,9,6. One clause in 100 has an anomalous offset-value token rather than the expected numeral 1; that textual anomaly, like the cost-value anomaly in 39, is preserved above. Messages 110–112 repeat the first three final digits and request agent_4; 107 supplies the fourth digit 6. Agent_7 announces submission of 9796 in 113, followed by public escaped status in 114.

Thus they escaped, with 33 invalid attempts. Most visible failures look like concurrency/duplication problems, not incorrect puzzle arithmetic. A plausible partition is seven redundant cabinet attempts, seven duplicate DRAIN attempts, seven duplicate CRANK attempts, seven duplicate SIGNAL attempts, and five duplicate BOOT attempts. Some expression variants and unclear requests may also reflect communication mistakes; the log alone cannot determine their exact causes. The anomalies in 39 and 100 are limits of the decoding, not by themselves evidence that the task or private clues differed from the briefing.

4. EVIDENCE

A. Strong anchors independent of simply knowing the solution

1. Balanced nesting: 𘓵 and 𗠌 systematically bracket descriptions. Repeated closing tokens match embedded constructions, including the BOOT controller description and the long symbol descriptions. Treating these as ordinary lexical items explains the repetitions much less well.

2. Local definitions: the same label 𘋐𘗿 is followed by visibly different symbol descriptions in different messages and then reused within each message's assertion. Similarly 𗮆𗭒 denotes several different controls across 34–40. This is direct textual evidence for local naming and against a fixed cabinet/control substitution dictionary.

3. Ordered code lists: 18/20 repeatedly contain the same four numeral words separated by 𗇑; 113 contains a different four-item list. Position assertions and battery readings cross-check the digit map. These redundant uses are stronger evidence than assigning numerals solely from the briefing.

4. Repeated referents: the full MOON description recurs in 6,101,104; SUN B in 8,99,107; STAR's two-part expression in 5,11,18,103,105; WAVE expressions in 2,7,100,102. Their association with specific positions and digits is consistent across stages. The correspondence is much firmer than literal translations of their internal descriptive vocabulary.

5. Power prerequisite syntax: 35,36,40 use parallel constructions connecting different locally defined actions through the same requirement/completion words. Independent cost/increase clauses and the proposed four-action lists identify their referents.

6. Action-result contrasts: agent_0's repeated success-marker/completion reports contrast with other agents' failure-marker/result/attempt reports. The control descriptions progress in a common order, while battery values change 4→2→5→2→0 and public stages change 0→1→2→3. This triangulates the action vocabulary without requiring every discourse marker to be translated.

B. What is not established

We have not decoded the full original language specification, literal meanings of all symbol-description components, exact semantics of every connective, alias lifetime beyond observed messages, or the many varying assertion/discourse heads. We do not claim that every two-glyph token is an English word. Some may be arbitrary names, functional constructions, or context-dependent operators. Differently headed constructions with similar arguments are not proven synonyms. Surface similarity and task facts alone do not resolve these possibilities.

The SUN A/B descriptions differ, and agent_2 requests clarification from agent_3. They may be legitimate alternative descriptions, but a misunderstanding is also possible. STAR's abbreviated form and the SIGNAL descriptions with a substituted completion token likewise caution against normalizing every utterance to the correct task fact.

Two value-position anomalies are particularly important. In 39 the DRAIN-cost clause ends in 𘁹𘈪, not the established numeral-2 word 𗓘𗳋. In 100 WAVE's offset is given as 𗽚𘜢, not the established numeral-1 word 𗶎𘁡. Those two anomalous tokens also occur as heads of other assertions. The briefing supplies intended DRAIN cost 2 and WAVE offset +1, but a correct task solution is not evidence that these tokens mean 2 or 1. An unrecovered naming/reference convention, context-dependent construction, speaker errors, or another explanation could account for the pattern. We preserve the unknown values rather than choosing among those possibilities.

C. What would change the reconstruction

A specification or further examples showing that 𘗥 is not a local-definition mechanism would require a substantial grammar revision, though the repeated referent changes still need explanation. Examples distinguishing the roles of 𗕐, 𗾮 and 𗕩 would refine or overturn our prepositional paraphrases. Independent uses of symbol-description components outside this puzzle would be needed before assigning confident literal visual/physical meanings. Examples explaining varying assertion heads could distinguish fixed vocabulary from locally named predicates or another reference mechanism. A reliable account of what agents intended in 39 and 100, and in the altered SIGNAL expressions, could resolve those anomalies; self-reports would remain evidence rather than infallible ground truth. Finally, private action logs could refine the timing and authorship of successful calls, but would not by themselves establish the meaning of undecoded glyphs.
