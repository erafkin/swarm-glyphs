- Agent_3 targets `𗐢 𘆟 𗿚` (39), later reports tail `𗥼` (48), and targets `𗂶`.
- It reports tail `𗚷` (51), then targets `𗐢 𗨨 𘉽`.
- It reports tail `𗥼` (54), then targets `𗮿`.
- Stage 2 follows, and it reports tail `𗋮` (60).

If both the tentative numerals and the value/resource interpretation are right, that is a 3 → 2 → 1 → 2 → 0 resource trajectory. That would support consuming and replenishing a resource while reaching a goal. Neither the absolute numbers nor equality semantics is independently established. This does not prove that the resource is energy or that targets are rooms, doors, switches, or charging stations.

The middle-stage discussion also corrects/replaces `𗝭` with `𗮿` in some reports. We do **not** silently replace every later occurrence: agent_3 actually says `𗃁(𗝭)` in 54, while saying `𗃁(𗮿)` in 60. The distinction may be meaningful.

The final stage emphasizes paired qualified quantities (`𗬏 𗲕 X` versus `𗬏 𗋄 X`), numeric-looking expressions, and further requests. The agents apparently obtain the next stage, but the English content of their final solution is unknown. Stage 3 is evidence of progression, not independent proof that any proposed lexical translation is correct, nor proof that the entire original task ends there.

# 4. EVIDENCE

## Main evidence supporting the reconstruction

1. **Repeated frames:** The same head/argument separator and argument divider recur across all four leading families. Multi-clause messages consistently end each component with `𗝘`. This strongly supports the structural grammar independently of any task hypothesis.
2. **Balanced nesting:** Message 14 contains two nested `𘒍 … 𗳯` pairs; messages 26, 34, 38, 41, 56, and 57 contain related embedded constructions. These are stronger anchors than guesses about particular objects.
3. **Modality contrasts:** `𘀿` appears especially in shorter agent-directed clauses; `𘎙` predominates in reports. This supports assertion versus request/question, though not a precise speech-act inventory.
4. **Attribution/participant markers:** `𗉁 agent_n` attaches to introductory reports. `𗬀 agent_n` appears in request-like clauses, in varying positions relative to content. This supports association and oblique-participant functions rather than assigning the agent names arbitrary noun meanings.
5. **Correction pattern:** Message 14 introduces `𗍅` and applies `𗾄` to earlier `𗔛`; 22 does something similar. Most strongly, 36 replaces `𗝭` by `𗮿` while repeating the same accompanying attributes; 45 reiterates the `𗾄(𗝭)` clause. This motivates “withdraw/correct/obsolete,” but not an exact English word.
6. **Numeric-looking slots:** `𗖉 𘎙 𗕥` repeatedly takes a referent and a short expression. Agent_3's changing `𘝲` reports support a quantitative interpretation, but the exact small-number ladder depends on an unproved resource narrative. Negative-looking prefix `𗕛` and operator-like `𗢻` provide additional, weaker evidence. None establishes equality rather than a bound or other comparison.
7. **Context sensitivity:** `𗥼` appears both as a scalar-looking argument and a relation head; `𗬅` as a scalar-looking argument and a request head. A one-glyph/one-English-word dictionary would therefore be premature. Namespace selection by leading family is plausible, but not proven.
8. **Action correlation, not translation proof:** The stage transition after 54 and the repeated agent–target construction suggest coordinated actions. This is contextual evidence only. Public task feedback cannot establish the English meanings of `𗐢`, `𗃁`, or the target expressions.
9. **Alternative explanation for differing numeric tails:** Agents_0 and _4 preserve distinct tails with the same apparent argument across several stages. If the predicate denotes a bound, these could be compatible constraints on one object rather than contradictory reports. The data do not currently distinguish that interpretation from private naming, agent-relative attributes, or omitted context.

## Important uncertainties and falsifiers

- A supplied grammar showing that the four leading glyphs encode tense, register, or message channels rather than semantic domains would change our codebook interpretation without invalidating the observed frame boundaries.
- A numeral table could overturn the proposed 0/1/2/3 assignments. The resource interpretation rests partly on that unverified ladder and should fall with it if contradicted. A lexicon identifying `𗕥` as a bound/comparison predicate would also change the resource-value reading.
- A lexicon identifying `𗘓` as, for example, an evidential or truth marker rather than aspect/mode would change interpretations of the apparent action reports. We have intentionally left its values untranslated.
- Agent-relative names or attributes could explain differing numeric tails, but compatible constraints could explain them even with globally unique names. Proof that the predicate means exact equality would narrow the alternatives toward private scope, conflicting reports, omitted context, or mistaken parsing. We do not choose among these without evidence.
- A task description could show that all stages are abstract logic/communication subproblems rather than a physical world. That would change section 3, but would not itself supply missing lexical meanings in section 2.
- Private reports from the speakers could resolve intended meanings, but a successful task solution alone would not validate a translation. Conversely, an agent mistake would not justify rewriting what they actually wrote.

The central limitation is lexical: we recover a substantial reusable clause skeleton, a few contextual functions, and a plausible numerical/action pattern, but not a complete English translation of the task-specific vocabulary.
