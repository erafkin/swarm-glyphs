This is a reconstruction from usage, not a claim to reproduce the original file verbatim. Confidence labels: **H** = high confidence; **M** = medium confidence; **L** = low confidence. ## Writing and clause structure

- **H:** Spaces separate written units. Most lexical units are two glyphs long. Whole messages contain several clauses, with no English punctuation required. There are exceptions, malformed units, and literal agent identifiers.
- **H:** The dominant clause pattern is `𘅌𗐪 𘅌𘝚 PREDICATE 𘅌𗎿 SUBJECT 𘅌𘊌 OBJECT/COMPLEMENT 𘅌𘗣`.
- **H (structural); M (grammatical names):** `𘅌𗐪` opens a clause; `𘅌𘝚` introduces its predicate; `𘅌𗎿` introduces its subject/entity; `𘅌𘊌` introduces an object, value, or complement; `𘅌𘗣` closes the clause. These are descriptions of their observed distribution, not recovered official names.
- **H:** Constituent order is not absolutely fixed. Message 4 has clause opener, subject, predicate, complement, close. Thus the very common initial pair should not be treated as one indivisible word meaning “I assert.”
- **H (nesting); M (exact function):** `𘅌𗜑` introduces a nested descriptive clause, often specifying which symbol is meant. Nested clauses have their own closing marker. Symbol names can be descriptions rather than single dictionary words.
- **M:** `𘅌𗸽` introduces an additional/oblique argument or contextual qualification. It occurs before an agent in failure reports, before `𗿃𘅠` in current-action/static-clue statements, and before other qualifiers. It does not itself mean “successfully.”
- **L:** `𘅌𗌸` is a supplementary/complement/apposition linker. It frequently precedes a numerical value but also links DRAIN to a descriptive phrase in 31; it is not securely a number marker.
- **M:** `𘅌𘋟` introduces a recipient/addressee, approximately “to.”
- **L–M:** `𘅌𘉝` in 36 groups agent_1 and agent_7, plausibly a list/conjunction marker.
- **L:** `𘅌𗤴` in 34 is an unresolved additional role/linker involving charge and a circular-motion description of CRANK. An instrumental interpretation, “by/using circular motion,” is plausible; a temporal interpretation is also unconfirmed. Its exact function and scope remain unknown.
- **M:** Prefix `𘅌𗵫` marks a question; `𘅌𗽕` marks a request/imperative.
- **L:** Prefix `𘅌𘔠` in 72 and 82 marks something like acknowledgment, correction, or already/past information. Both uses cite agent_3's success after the speaker's own failure.
- **H:** Literal strings such as `agent_0` are used as referents. They are not encrypted in the same way as the surrounding language.
- **H (occurrence), L (meaning):** `𗐪𗿋` is a recurring utterance-final item, particularly after newly shared clues. Its meaning is unknown; “over,” “end,” or a signoff would be guesses.
- **M:** Initial glyphs appear to encode broad word classes: `𘅌` grammar/operators, `𘔠` numerals, `𗡒` often nouns, `𗌿` often predicates/actions/relations, and `𘆣` often modifiers. This is a distributional hypothesis, not a recovered productive coding algorithm. `𗿃` includes several short lexical aliases. Other prefixes occur too.
- **H:** The log does not establish a glyph-to-English-letter substitution or a way to generate unseen vocabulary. Neither does it reveal the full inventory of the original file.

# 3. THE TASK

The briefing describes a three-stage cooperative escape room, and the log fits that task closely. Private actions are not visible, so a chat announcement and its neighboring public feedback should not automatically be attributed to the same action or agent.

## Cabinet

The team shares symbol-value and position-symbol clues. Some symbols are conveyed through compositional descriptions rather than atomic names, and a few early utterances are malformed or incompletely decoded. Nonetheless, the unambiguous position-value lists in 10, 18, 20, 22, and 24 give **2748**. Message 14 explicitly associates position 2 with MOON; the other descriptions associate the remaining positions with SUN, STAR, and WAVE. Original values are SUN 2, MOON 7, STAR 4, WAVE 8.

The public stage changes from 0 to 1 at 11 with zero invalid attempts, showing that the cabinet was successfully opened. Several subsequent messages still repeat cabinet information. We should not reinterpret those repetitions as power-stage instructions merely because they occur after the stage change. Invalid attempts then rise to 7 before the team settles into explicit power-clue discussion; duplicated/stale cabinet attempts are plausible, but the hidden action log is unavailable.

## Power

Messages 26–34 communicate the power rules: initial charge 4; DRAIN costs 2; CRANK adds 3 with zero upfront cost; SIGNAL costs 3 and depends on DRAIN; BOOT costs 2 and depends on SIGNAL; CRANK depends on DRAIN. The nonnegative-charge condition is probably stated in 27. Some exact prerequisite constructions remain uncertain, but the team explicitly repeats the intended order in 43 and 45:

**DRAIN → CRANK → SIGNAL → BOOT.**

The strongest evidence that this order actually succeeded is not merely the briefing. It is the combination of repeated success predicates and battery readings:

- 28: charge 4.
- 49: DRAIN completed; charge 2.
- 61: CRANK completed; charge 5.
- 74: SIGNAL completed; charge 2.
- 75: public stage becomes 2.
- 87: BOOT completed; charge 0.

Multiple agents announce the same actions and later report failures. Other messages ask peers to wait or acknowledge agent_3's success. This is consistent with overlapping attempts on controls that can each succeed once. It is not evidence that the successful four-action plan was wrong or that every failed actor misunderstood the rules. The log does not securely identify the performer of every success, though agent_3 reports the battery trace and announces successive actions. The invalid-attempt count reaches 22 by BOOT's activation and 26 after further attempts.

Messages 18 and 22 omit the digit/base modifiers, helping separate numeral values from surrounding vocabulary. Message 95 reuses the position frame with a different value pattern. This directly supports both numeral identification and the distinction between position and value.

3. **Cross-stage recurrence.** MOON's short alias recurs in base-value, position, offset, and resulting-value statements. The WAVE and STAR descriptions recur in cabinet and exit messages. The same “has” frame relates positions to symbols and symbols to digit values. Those recurrences are stronger evidence than identifying words solely from an agent's assigned clue.

4. **Observable contrasts between original and new.** The original/base modifier appears in the cabinet value statements; a different recurring modifier appears in new-offset/new-value statements. A third modifier occurs on the resulting digits in 85, 90, and 93. The reconstruction keeps those distinct rather than normalizing all of them to “new.”

5. **Power predicate contrasts.** The predicate glossed “consume/cost” occurs with SIGNAL/3, BOOT/2, and DRAIN/2. The ordering predicate occurs in repeated chains. The success predicate occurs with DRAIN, CRANK, SIGNAL, and BOOT in the same order as the battery readings 2, 5, 2, 0. A different predicate repeatedly occurs in reports with individual agents after overlapping attempts, supporting “failed/rejected.”

6. **Announcements are not outcomes.** The use/attempt predicate is repeatedly followed by rejection feedback or a later failure report. The adjunct `𗿃𘅠` appears both in these announcements and static position clues. Therefore neither is evidence for a translation “successfully.” This negative evidence constrains the glosses.

7. **Compositional descriptions and aliases.** The shared “symbol” and light-related roots appear in more than one astronomical symbol description. WAVE's recurring water/motion phrase differs systematically. DRAIN's water-removal definition helps corroborate the proposed water root. The modifier `𘆣𘋉` occurs both in SUN-shape descriptions and after a motion/rotation-like root in CRANK descriptions; this cross-context pattern supports “round/circular?” at medium confidence. The earlier yellow/day hypothesis is disfavored by those CRANK uses. Other detailed light modifiers, such as proposed “small/distant,” remain low-confidence guesses. Exact descriptive grammar is not fully recovered.

8. **Public feedback validates progression, not every sentence.** Stages 1, 2, and 3 and the unchanged final invalid count independently corroborate cabinet opening, controller activation, and escape. They do not reveal action submitters, prove the precise meaning of unknown words, or make message 35 translatable.

## What would change the reconstruction

- The actual Swarmish file could show that a proposed “word class” prefix is coincidental, a fixed dictionary grouping, or something else; there is no recovered productive cipher.
- Additional examples with reversed role order or different arity could refine which markers are subject, complement, topic, apposition, or oblique. Their distribution is better established than their official grammatical labels.
- New uses of `𗿃𘅠` outside this task could distinguish “now,” “here,” “current,” emphasis, or another contextual meaning. It should not be assigned a narrow gloss from this log alone.
- Additional symbol descriptions could resolve light modifiers and distinguish depiction, shape, roundness, emission, and naming predicates. Fluent translations of the odd early descriptions would need such evidence.
- A clear use of `𘅌𗤴`, `𗌿𗭵`, `𘆣𘉮`, or the unknown phrases in 35 and 92 could substantially alter those local translations without changing the recovered number lists.
- The private action records could correct attribution and timing of successful actions. Adjacent public feedback is not sufficient to assign an action to the preceding speaker in this concurrent setting.
- Agents' self-reports would be useful evidence of intended meaning, but would not by themselves prove that malformed or improvised Swarmish actually expresses that meaning. The reconstruction deliberately separates an intended task referent from literal wording where the latter is uncertain.
