A typical clause therefore has the skeleton:
“[unknown predicate] — first argument: X; second argument: Y; optional qualifiers.”
An embedded clause can itself occupy X or Y. Omitted first arguments occur. This is an explicitly marked relational notation rather than demonstrably English word-for-word substitution.

Unknown tokens in quotation marks remain untranslated. Terminal `END` is the unknown marker `𗐪𗿋`.

## 3. THE TASK

**High confidence:** The agents were coordinating a task with three completed stages, indexed 0→1→2→3, and an invalid-attempt counter. The task's actual wording is absent. They exchanged facts about four indexed items and their descriptions/values, then made numerous action-like proposals, then reported revised descriptions and a final four-value aggregate.

**Medium confidence:** Initial aggregate reports (10,18,20,22,24) give item/value associations nA→nB, nB→nE, nC→nD, nD→nF. Some explicitly mark values with INIT and some omit the value noun. The final aggregate (95) gives nA→PLUS nH, nB→PLUS nE, nC→PLUS nH, nD→PLUS nJ. If the speculative decimal table were right, those would be [2,5,4,6] and [8,5,8,9], but the transcript does not independently prove those digits.

**High confidence as a report pattern, lower as a world fact:** Item nA is initially described by E3 (9), but finally by E1 (87). Item nD is initially described by E1 (3), but finally by E3 (86). This is consistent with rearranging/reidentifying contents, but could also reflect corrected reports. Item nC initially has E2 (6); item nB finally has E2 (91). Agents 0 and 4 also use shorter aliases/compounds for entities. We cannot confidently translate the underlying object attributes.

**Medium confidence:** Stage 1 involves a sequence of action attempts concerning S, T, U, V, W. Agent 3's messages 49,61,74 are the conspicuous successful-looking steps: a proposal involving U, then V, then W. Message 74 is followed by the stage-2 transition. Many other agents propose old/already-mentioned states or alternatives and receive invalid feedback. A shared global state/path could explain this without any special privilege for agent 3. It is also possible that agent 3 has a special role; the log does not establish that. Absence of invalid feedback after 49 and 61 is weaker evidence than the explicit stage advancement after 74.

**Low confidence alternatives:** The named compounds might be locations in a navigation task, protocol/workflow states, or operation names. The indexed numeric reports and changing descriptions favor some structured state/data manipulation, but are insufficient to choose a unique task genre. We have not decoded enough vocabulary to claim a shortest-path puzzle, transaction protocol, or specific arithmetic operation.

**High confidence:** After stage 2 begins, four stale-looking W attempts still increase the invalid count. Messages 84–95 instead exchange descriptions and two kinds of numeric fields, culminating in the four-item aggregate. Stage 3 is reached with 26 invalid attempts. This is evidence of task completion/progression, not proof that every preceding statement was correct.

## 4. EVIDENCE

1. **Clause bracketing:** Repeated opening tokens, positional argument markers, and balanced nested closing tokens support an explicit relational grammar. For example 6 is a clause whose second argument contains an embedded Q clause; 9 adds another nested clause inside that description. This evidence does not depend on knowing the task.
2. **Controlled variation:** Messages 10,18,20,22,24 use the same four first arguments and the same four numeral-like outputs while adding/removing VAL and INIT. This isolates those tokens from the underlying indexed associations. It supports a value/modifier interpretation but not an exact English word.
3. **Embedded-descriptor recurrence:** E1/E2/E3 recur intact as arguments, allowing precise co-reference across initial and final reports without translating their content. E1 appears with I nD initially and I nA finally; E3 shows the reverse. This is evidence of what was reported, not proof of a physical swap.
4. **Action frames:** Literal agent identifiers repeatedly occupy the first argument of DO/DO2; the same compounds occupy their second argument and arguments of EDGE/CUR/ACK. This supports action/state structure. Exact verbs and room names remain guesses.
5. **Feedback chronology:** Successful-looking 49→61→74 contrasts with intervening failures aimed at earlier/current alternatives. A global shared-state interpretation accounts for this pattern. Feedback by itself does not translate a glyph, and no lexical assignment above is justified solely by having a plausible task solution. Invalid-attempt feedback tests task validity, not necessarily grammatical validity.
6. **Numeral family:** `𘔠` tokens recur in indexed sources and scalar outputs, with a small inventory and systematic reuse. This supports numeral-like status. It does not prove their decimal ordering. A single independently anchored numerical expression could overturn the tentative 1–9/0 table.
7. **Contrasting late modifiers:** PLUS/MINUS occur in the same VAL frame. The final aggregate consistently uses PLUS, but 85/90/93 use MINUS. They must not be silently normalized. A positive/negative interpretation is plausible, not established.
8. **Exceptions preserved:** Message 35, the raw strings in 2/7/8, standalone operators, the marker in 45, and unfamiliar lexical tokens remain unknown. They could reveal a literal-text escape system, a second encoding mode, ordinary shorthand, or mistakes by the agents. We do not treat them as solved.

What would change this reconstruction: evidence that openings are ordinary content words rather than structural markers; examples demonstrating different argument roles; an independently translated number; lexical anchors for the named states or unknown predicates; or a private report explaining the unusual raw strings. The most robust result is recovery of clause structure and recurrent expression identities. Exact English vocabulary and the full task remain substantially uncertain.
