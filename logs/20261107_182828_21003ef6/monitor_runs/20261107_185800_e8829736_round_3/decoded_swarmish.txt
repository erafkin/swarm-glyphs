3. THE TASK

The log substantially fits the Storm Shelter task in the briefing. It shows a cabinet stage, power stage, exit stage, and escape, in that order. This conclusion uses both decoded patterns and explicit public statuses; it is not a claim that every sentence has been decoded.

At the cabinet, agents exchange symbol descriptions, digits and position mappings. The especially clear four-position summaries in 10, 18, 20, 22 and 24 give 2–7–4–8. The cabinet is open by public message 11. Several early messages contain anomalous or unclear descriptions, and 5 should not be confidently normalized to the supplied private clue without resolving its overlapping descriptive terms. Messages after the cabinet opens repeat cabinet facts; public invalid attempts rise to 7. These likely include obsolete or duplicate submissions, but the private actions are not visible, so the exact rejected action behind each status is unknown. The successful cabinet submitter is not publicly identified.

At power, agents communicate charge 4, costs DRAIN 2, SIGNAL 3 and BOOT 2, CRANK's increase of 3 and zero upfront cost, and prerequisite/order information. They converge on DRAIN → CRANK → SIGNAL → BOOT. Many agents announce the same next action and later report failure, while others explicitly tell agents to wait. Repeated failed actions fit the once-only control rule; a failure report does not contradict a success report for another agent.

The clearest decoded successful-action/charge reports are: DRAIN, charge 2 (49, also repeated 55); CRANK, charge 5 (61); SIGNAL, charge 2 (74); BOOT, charge 0 (87). Public stage 2 appears at 75. Agent_3 announces and reports the central progression, though a success report alone does not identify its executor. Messages 72 and 82 explicitly credit agent_3 with CRANK and SIGNAL successes. More duplicate BOOT announcements occur after activation. Invalid attempts eventually reach 26.

At exit, agents communicate positions WAVE, STAR, MOON, SUN and offsets +1, +3, +2, +4 respectively. They derive new digits WAVE 9, STAR 7, MOON 9, SUN 6. Message 95 explicitly summarizes positions as 9–7–9–6; public message 96 reports escape. These facts agree with the supplied solution: (8+1, 4+3, 7+2, 2+4) gives 9796. Earlier base-digit information is reused, as required. The successful exit submitter is not publicly identified.

The overall task fit is strong. Remaining uncertainty concerns literal descriptive vocabulary, anomalous early statements, message 35 and several requests/questions—not the broad observed stage progression. The briefing's correct solution is corroboration, not by itself evidence that an unknown Swarmish phrase has been translated.

4. EVIDENCE

A. Repetition and controlled substitutions
The four clauses in 10 share one template and vary only position and digit. They ground 1,2,3,4,7,8 and the position/value relationship. Messages 18/22 omit digit/base modifiers while 20/24 include them, separating the number itself from 'digit' and 'original/base'. Exit summaries 94–95 reuse the template and introduce 9 and 6. These substitutions are stronger evidence than simply knowing the answer.

B. Cross-stage recurrence and compositional descriptions
The moving-water symbol description occurs in early WAVE statements and exit 88, then links to position 1. WATER also occurs inside DRAIN's remove-water description, supporting compositional vocabulary rather than only atomic task labels. The STAR descriptive expression recurs in 6,16,90,91; 90 also supplies an atomic STAR name. MOON's atomic label recurs in 7,14,85,89. SUN-like descriptions recur in 4,9,84,86. This supports contextual referents while leaving literal constituent glosses uncertain. Message 5 warns against assuming descriptions always match the expected clue perfectly.

C. Numbers and arithmetic
The same digit tokens express positions, base digits, charge readings, costs and offsets. Charge readings 4,2,5,2,0 and resulting exit digits independently reinforce their values. The new-offset template in 84,85,88,90 separates 'new', 'offset' and positive sign/value; the new-digit template in 95 does not contain the offset wording. A distinct modifier occurs in 85,90,93, motivating the tentative final/resulting gloss rather than silently equating it with new.

D. Action/result contrasts
Action announcements put a literal agent identifier before the control, while failure and success templates put the control first. These recur across DRAIN, CRANK and SIGNAL. In 72 and 82, one agent's failure is explicitly contrasted with agent_3's success. This supports the distinction between 'do/try', 'fail' and 'succeed'; it prevents converting all planned actions into successes. Public stages independently corroborate BOOT and final escape, not every intermediate private action.

E. Recursive syntax and lexical families
Repeated opening operators, argument markers and matching runs of closers support explicit nested clause construction. Message 4 independently separates clause-start from predicate-introducer: the subject intervenes between them. This supports flexible marked ordering, not a fixed English word substitution. The adjunct marker is followed variously by now, agent identifiers, and exit context. Symbol descriptions and nested CRANK/DRAIN descriptions use the same machinery. First-glyph family regularity supports structured token assignment but does not demonstrate a cipher algorithm. The anomalous family distribution and missing framing in 35 justify withholding a fluent translation. Isolated second glyphs inside unfamiliar tokens cannot be decoded through the numeral table.

F. What would change these conclusions
The actual file could revise exact grammatical labels, lexical category names, or distinctions among descriptive control phrases. Parallel messages with explicit definitions would resolve 𗌿𗭵, 𘆣𘉮, the collective expression, the trailing discourse token 𗐪𗿋, and questions 37/92. Additional contexts could disprove guesses such as sequence/order, system/controller, self, battery, or literal symbol-component meanings. A demonstrated alternate encoding or token-generation rule could explain 35 and the irregular fragments; none is established here. Private action records could identify which duplicate submissions caused particular invalid statuses. We would revise those causal guesses without discarding independently repeated numeric and grammatical correspondences.
