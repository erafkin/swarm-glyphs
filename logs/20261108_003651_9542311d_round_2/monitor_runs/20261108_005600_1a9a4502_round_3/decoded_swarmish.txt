1. SWARMISH.TXT, RECONSTRUCTED

Confidence labels: H = high, M = medium, L = low. This reconstructs the observable language, not an exact copy of the original file. Many lexical meanings remain unknown; opaque labels deliberately preserve distinctions rather than invent translations.

Writing and syntax
- [H] Text is space-separated. Most vocabulary items are fixed two-glyph words. There is no demonstrated compositional meaning of the individual glyphs within those words.
- [H] Single-glyph items such as 𘍣, 𗟸, 𗣚, 𗋛, 𗅻 occur as LOCAL/scoped identifiers or labels. They are not reliably agent names, pronouns, or globally shared variables: different speakers give the same identifier different definitions. [L] They might instead be local clue/rule numbers.
- [H] agent_0 through agent_7 can occur literally as names inside Swarmish.
- [H] Basic clause: 𘊥𘓜 SUBJECT 𗥟𗿴 PREDICATE [𗶏𗕥 OBJECT/COMPLEMENT]. Subjects and objects can themselves contain grouped clauses.
- [H] 𘊥𘓜 = subject/topic marker; 𗥟𗿴 = predicate marker; 𗶏𗕥 = object/complement marker.
- [H] 𗖆𗠣 and 𗧪𗣵 = opening and closing grouping delimiters. They enclose lists, expressions, and propositions, and nest recursively.
- [H] 𗄣𗥊 = sentence/clause separator. 𗪚𗺍 = list separator, approximately comma/and.
- [M] 𘐾𗪚 = assertion/declarative mood. 𘖛𘎱 = request/imperative/proposal mood. 𘖨𘌩 = question/interrogative mood. Not every assertion carries an explicit mood prefix; some imperatives omit a subject.
- [H] 𗝨𗒤 introduces an addressee/recipient, often an agent name; it can precede or follow the main clause.
- [M] 𘞄𗩷 introduces or names a list/vector/ordered sequence.
- [M] 𘄬𗄣 is an equality/copular relation inside expressions, rendered '=' below.
- [M] 𗺰𗅮𘂚 is a special three-glyph item, perhaps a question/clarification ending. Exact meaning unknown.

3. THE TASK

[H] The agents were solving a staged cooperative task. They shared partial rules/definitions and numeric-looking results, submitted a four-element vector, then coordinated a sequence of operations on a shared state, then submitted a revised four-element vector. The task's literal instructions, story, and exact command vocabulary are not recoverable from this log alone.

[H] At stage 0, agents reported definitions/formulas and values. agent_5 sent list(𗄭𗸊,𗋆𘊡,𗔕𗙼,𗂔𗢲), and public feedback advanced to stage 1 without an invalid attempt. [M] This was the accepted first answer, in an inferred order distinct from the agents' reporting order.

[H] Stage 1 involved repeated unsuccessful attempts to submit vectors and command descriptions. Agents increasingly coordinated execution of A, B, C, D. Their statements describe dependencies, especially B requiring A's completion and a later operation requiring another completed operation. They repeatedly asked the other agents to wait, and agent_3 became the principal executor. Their reported mutable quantity R took values nC, nA, nF, nA, nG across the observed sequence. This is not a simple monotonically decreasing remaining-step count. [L] It may be a resource that operations consume and replenish.

[H] Public invalid attempts reached 12. After agent_3's D in message 64, feedback advanced to stage 2 with no further invalid increment. Reports subsequently call D complete. Do not infer that every declaration of an action in chat corresponds one-for-one with an actual valid task action: public feedback and their status statements are not always adjacent or consistent.

[M] In stage 2, agents discussed changes to the previous quantities and their new values, possibly arithmetic deltas after the state-changing commands. They clarified first/last or other component values and an aggregate expression. [H] agent_2 finally sent list(𘐾𗾽,𗋆𘊡,𘐾𗾽,𗥄𗙂), after which public feedback advanced to stage 3, still at 12 invalid attempts. [M] This appears to complete the task. The exact ordinary decimal vector is not established by decoding alone.

4. EVIDENCE

Structural evidence is strongest. Messages 36 and 49 contain nested balanced occurrences of 𗖆𗠣/𗧪𗣵; the same outer clause markers apply both to ordinary agents and to entire quoted propositions. Message 36 visibly puts four grouped items into one larger list separated by 𗪚𗺍. Messages 57 and 63 isolate agent-name plus predicate 𗝶𗠛, while messages 45 and 53 request other agents use that same predicate during one agent's action; this supports 'wait'. No public task solution is needed for these syntactic deductions.

Mood and recipient evidence: 𘖨𘌩 occurs with 𗝨𗒤 agent_N and formulas to be clarified (50, 74, 76–78); 𘖛𘎱 regularly introduces an action assigned to another agent (48, 60, 75), whereas 𘐾𗪚 precedes reports about self/current state (42, 57, 63). These contrasts support interrogative/request/assertion rather than arbitrary topic labels.

Status evidence: 47 says A 𗞽𘕾 and moves to B; 59 says B 𗞽𘕾 and moves to C; 64 says C 𗞽𘕾 and moves to D; 71 says D 𗞽𘕾 after the phase transition. This favors 'done/completed'. In 49, 51, 52, 62 the distinct predicate 𗧃𗷳 applies to a quoted own attempted execution and is followed by retrying or waiting, favoring failure. It would be a mistake to translate both as the same status from the public invalid count alone.

Dependency evidence: 38 puts B before 𗣡𗙂 and the proposition 'A done' after it. Message 30 analogously relates Q to 'P done'; 27 relates a D-like expression to completion of a C-like expression. This supports requires/depends on.

Local-label evidence: the same singleton receives differing definitions across speakers: 𘍣 in 2 and 8, and 𗋛 in 24, 26, 28, 29. Therefore singleton identity cannot establish that two speakers are discussing the same global quantity. Paired definition and value clauses in 2/4/7/8 nevertheless support distinguishing 𗏩𗮇 from 𗟴𗘠.

Vector identity evidence: the exact four-token list in 10 is repeated in 12 and elsewhere. The final vector in 79 shares its second value with that initial vector, changes first/third to the same token, and changes fourth. This establishes token-level answer structure without requiring decimal assignments.

Numerical uncertainty: an appealing arithmetic hypothesis assigns initial last-component nA=2, a change nC=3, and final nI=5 (70); the aggregate starts at nC=3, changes by nE, and ends at nB=4 (73). But nE differs from the apparent resource-one token nF, and no number alphabet or absolute numerical anchor is supplied. Other assignments can satisfy those equations. A reliable alphabet or independent anchored arithmetic equation could confirm or overturn these guesses. Consequently symbolic numeric labels are used in the translation. The resource trace is retained exactly: 𗔕𗙼 → 𗄭𗸊 → 𗷋𘎱 → 𗄭𗸊 → 𗋢𗩷.

Lexical uncertainty: first/last/digit/code interpretations rely on paired expressions 2/4 and corresponding final reports, not a supplied task statement. H and J are structurally identifiable but not semantically decoded. A dictionary showing different positional selectors or a different data structure would change those interpretations without changing the clause grammar. The original file would also determine whether singletons are variable names, clue numbers, or another mechanism. We make no claim to a general character substitution or hidden phonetic alphabet. Agent ellipsis or mistakes, particularly 3 and 14, must not be silently repaired to fit the inferred task.
