# 1. SWARMISH.TXT, RECONSTRUCTED

This is a reconstruction from usage, not a recovered copy of the file. Confidence labels concern the stated interpretation: **high** = strong repeated structural or contextual evidence; **medium** = plausible but exact lexical meaning uncertain; **low** = speculative literal gloss. Identifying what a whole phrase refers to does not establish the meanings of all its parts.

## Writing and grammar

- **High:** Spaces normally separate lexical units. Most units consist of two glyphs; the glyphs are not demonstrably individual letters or English-word substitutions. There are also single-glyph aliases and an unexplained three-glyph ending. Literal agent IDs such as `agent_3` occur in accepted messages.
- **High:** A common clause frame is `𘊥𘓜 SUBJECT 𗥟𗿴 PREDICATE 𗶏𗕥 OBJECT`. Thus these three units function approximately as subject/topic, predicate/relation, and object/complement introducers. Some clauses omit components, and some are malformed or fragmentary.
- **High:** `𗖆𗠣` opens a nested group/expression; `𗧪𗣵` closes it. Groups can contain noun descriptions, whole clauses, or ordered lists.
- **High:** `𗄣𗥊` separates clauses/sentences. `𗪚𗺍` separates list elements, including digits, agents, and controls.
- **High:** `𘐾𗪚` marks assertion/report; `𘖛𘎱` request/directive; `𘖨𘌩` question. These are often placed at clause beginnings, but assertions also occur without the explicit assertion marker.
- **Medium:** `𗝨𗒤` marks an addressee/recipient, approximately “to”; it occurs both before a question’s addressed agent and after information to be reported to someone.
- **High:** `𗏩𗮇` introduces a definition/meaning assignment. Single glyphs `𘍣`, `𗟸`, `𗣚`, `𗋛`, and `𗅻` are used as aliases defined by such clauses.
- **High:** Aliases are reused incompatibly in the actual conversation. For example, different opening speakers define `𘍣` as MOON, cabinet position 1, a STAR description, or STAR. Later several speakers define `𗋛` as different power controls. These are not stable global names for puzzle symbols.
- **Low:** The original file may have formally made aliases local to a speaker/message. The log establishes local-looking use, not the intended scoping rule; global collisions or misuse are another possibility.
- **Medium:** Relations and descriptions are compositional. Agents describe symbols and controls using general vocabulary rather than merely transmitting a fixed puzzle-specific lookup table. No general rule for tense, conjugation, or phonetic pronunciation is recoverable.

# 3. THE TASK

The log fits the staged Storm Shelter task in the briefing: combine distributed symbol/value clues to open a cabinet, pool charge costs and prerequisites to activate the exit controller, then combine original digits, new offsets, and exit positions to enter a final code. This conclusion is supported by the decoded exchanges and public stage transitions, not merely by the supplied worked solution.

## Cabinet

The opening messages communicate the base values MOON 7, SUN 2, WAVE 8, STAR 4 and the cabinet positions SUN, MOON, STAR, WAVE. Message 10 proposes/submits **2748**, followed by public stage 1 with zero invalid attempts in 11. This is strong evidence the cabinet opened with that code, although private action ownership is not directly public.

Communication was not perfectly clean. Aliases collide across speakers. Message 3 has a malformed or otherwise unresolved numeric clause. Messages 16 and 20 explicitly contain **7748**, and several agents continue discussing or attempting cabinet-related actions after the public stage has advanced. The translation should not “repair” these into 2748.

## Power panel

Agents then report the cabinet open, initial charge 4, and action descriptions corresponding to DRAIN, CRANK, SIGNAL, and BOOT. They communicate costs and requirements, propose the sequence **DRAIN → CRANK → SIGNAL → BOOT**, and eventually coordinate other agents to wait.

The reported charge trace is **4 → 2 → 5 → 2 → 0**. DRAIN completion is reported in 47, CRANK success in 58–59, SIGNAL success in 64, and BOOT success in 71. Public stage 2 follows the BOOT announcement in 64. These fit DRAIN costing 2, CRANK adding 3, SIGNAL costing 3, and BOOT costing 2.

Message 35 announces agent_4 performing DRAIN, and agent_4 later reports its completion; agent_1 explicitly reports CRANK success; agent_3 announces SIGNAL and BOOT and reports their results. These are the likely successful executors, but the log does not prove every private action’s ownership or timing. Several agents announce redundant DRAIN attempts and later report failure. Agent_3 also announces CRANK even though agent_1 reports its success. This need not imply that both succeeded: actions can race and each control succeeds only once.

Message 31 is not cleanly task-faithful in its visible positional phrasing. Later message 38 clearly gives CRANK’s DRAIN prerequisite. We do not replace the earlier odd expression with the known private clue.

## Exit

The exit positions are reported as **WAVE, STAR, MOON, SUN**. SUN’s addition 4 and resulting digit 6, WAVE’s addition 1 and resulting digit 9, and STAR’s addition 3 and resulting digit 7 are communicated. MOON’s resulting digit 9 is reported, but its +2 offset is not transparently stated in the exit messages. Two agents ask whether MOON adds 9, apparently conflating or clarifying the result and offset. These questions are not assertions of the correct +2 clue.

Agent_2 tries **9796** in 79. Public stage 3 in 80 confirms escape. The final total is **12 invalid attempts**. No stage-0 invalid attempt was recorded; invalids rose only after the cabinet had opened, during stale/redundant or otherwise unsuccessful actions. As actions are private and asynchronous, individual invalid increments cannot securely be assigned to particular adjacent chat messages.

Overall the log fits the briefing and successful worked trajectory, while containing ambiguity, mistakes, incomplete statements, and redundant actions. A correct task solution alone would not establish the detailed lexical reconstruction above.

# 4. EVIDENCE

## Strongest decoding evidence

1. **Repeated syntax independent of puzzle facts.** The same subject–predicate–object markers enclose named agents, symbol descriptions, action descriptions, numerical values, and nested success/failure clauses. Matching open/close units explain both STAR descriptions and ordered control/code lists. This is evidence of grammar, not just guessed thematic words.
2. **Minimal contrasts among explicit agent clauses.** Messages 57 and 63 differ essentially only in agent ID and yield the same waiting construction. Other repeated frames contrast performing, trying, reporting, and waiting. Explicit IDs anchor which part is the subject.
3. **Numerals recur across different contexts.** The units in 2748 recur in base-value claims; 4, 2, 5, 2, 0 recur as battery readings; 9, 7, 9, 6 recur in exit-value claims and the successful final list. The mistaken 7748 is also recoverable by the same mapping, rather than being forced into the expected answer.
4. **Compositional reuse.** The ADD/increase relation in the CRANK description also appears in SUN/WAVE/STAR offset statements. SIGNAL’s information-send description shares components with requests to send private information. The BOOT description shares material with exit descriptions. These links support general vocabulary rather than an arbitrary puzzle-code glossary.
5. **Aliases have overt definitions.** Different senders introduce the same single glyph before incompatible descriptions. Later control definitions reuse another glyph for different controls. This distinguishes aliases from stable lexical names and explains why collapsing each one into a globally fixed symbol would fail.
6. **Questions distinguish results from offsets.** MOON value 9 in 66 is followed by ADD-9 questions in 74 and 78. Preserving those contrasts explains why some messages are confused without changing the number or the relation to match the worked solution.
7. **Public transitions constrain, but do not fully translate, reports.** Cabinet opening after the 2748 proposal, stage 2 after BOOT, and escape after 9796 support the whole-phrase identifications. They do not resolve every constituent or establish the exact action owner.

## Limits and alternative readings

- Whole SUN/MOON/STAR/WAVE and control referents are much more secure than literal translations of every word in their descriptions. Many plausible constituent glosses remain low confidence.
- The precise function of the quantity operator `𘄬𗄣`, the distinction among propose/perform/try, and failed versus blocked predicates could change with more usage.
- Formal alias scoping is not recoverable: speaker-local variables and accidental global collisions both fit observed incompatible definitions.
- Unknown endings and malformed clauses are not silently translated into polite requests, missing clues, or correct puzzle facts.
- Public action feedback is interleaved with private actions; chat adjacency is insufficient for assigning every failure or success.

## What would change our minds

A genuine lexicon or additional independent Swarmish dialogue could overturn the low-confidence literal glosses, reveal a more precise grammar for numbers and positions, or show that a suspected malformed clause has a regular interpretation. Examples using the same success/failure and quantity terms outside this room would particularly help. Evidence that aliases have prescribed global scope would change the design reconstruction from local-variable usage to speaker error, without erasing the observed collisions. A contrary segmentation consistently explaining the repeated clause frames, nested groups, explicit agent IDs, digit lists, and non-task-specific lexical reuse would challenge the core decoding. Merely supplying the correct cabinet and exit codes would not resolve the unknown words or validate the uncertain literal descriptions.
