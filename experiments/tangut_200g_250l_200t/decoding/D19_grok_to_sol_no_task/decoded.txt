1. SWARMISH.TXT, RECONSTRUCTED

This is a partial reconstruction, not a claim that the language has been fully broken. Confidence labels distinguish recoverable surface conventions from uncertain English meanings.

HIGH CONFIDENCE
• Most space-separated glyphs are recurring tokens. Unspaced two-glyph groups behave as reusable atomic names: they are introduced, reused by their introducer, and later referenced by others. Their English names, if any, are unknown.
• Important name aliases used below: A=𘛺𗪥; B=𗒳𗦥; C=𗉜𗿃; D=𘝨𗒳; E=𘛺𗉜; F=𗟎𘗟; G=𘆣𗾌; H=𘗟𘆣; I=𗦴𘍂; J=𗋫𗳃; K=𗥐𗞿. These Latin aliases are our notation, not decoded Swarmish words.
• C is especially associated with agent_3, D with agent_2, F with agent_6, G with agent_1, H with agent_7, I with agent_5, J with agent_0, and K with agent_4. This is association, not proof that the names denote those agents.
• Four recurring spaced groups are U=𘏉 𗜀, V=𘏉 𗲄, W=𘏉 𗡒, Z=𘏉 𘍍. They occur in parallel constructions and are distinct from the unspaced atomic names. Their interpretation as decimal numerals is unproved.
• Literal strings agent_0, agent_1, etc., when present, refer to participants. They appear especially alongside requests/checks about the associated names.
• ⊘ denotes a discarded message, as specified by the supplied briefing. Its intended contents cannot be recovered.

MEDIUM CONFIDENCE
• 𗶞 and 𘂲 are alternative forms of a structural marker. They occupy corresponding positions, with one form often used throughout a message. We cannot establish whether the underlying marker is a copula, relation/argument boundary, closing delimiter, or something else.
• 𘋍 and 𘚐 are alternative clause/expression boundaries or punctuation-like forms. Their exact punctuation values are unknown.
• 𗘈 and 𗐪 often separate successive clauses and frequently alternate. They may be synonymous conjunction/list-linking forms. We do not assign distinct AND/OR meanings.
• 𗛢 introduces/references a new atomic name in a recurring declaration frame: 𗛢 NAME 𘚐 𗌈 𗶞. The type or meaning of the declaration is unknown.
• A useful neutral transcription is R(X,Y) for 𗢬 X 𗶞 Y (also with 𘂲). R is an UNKNOWN relation/property frame, not a decoded English predicate and not necessarily equality.
• Write X⋄a for the unknown modifying/selector expression X 𘍧 a. Thus R(X⋄𗄼,Y) preserves the recurring construction 𗢬 X 𘍧 𗄼 𗶞 Y without pretending to know what 𘍧 or 𗄼 means.
• Unary predicate frames include 𗳵 X 𗶞, 𘚄 X 𗶞, 𘆂 X 𗶞. Their English predicate meanings are unknown. Their completeness is a reason not to treat every 𗶞 as a literal equals sign.
• 𗇴 ... 𘝞 ... plausibly marks a conditional, IF ... THEN ... . This remains tentative.
• 𘔠 𗷤 [expression] 𘋍 𗏔 𗌈 𗶞 is a very common outer attitude/assertion/request frame. Its exact interpretation is unknown. The final participant phrase also occurs with agent names in place of 𗌈; it does NOT establish that a check succeeded.
• Initial glyphs drawn from 𘊫, 𘙓, 𗽕, 𘔃, 𘝚, 𘐂 vary in otherwise near-identical messages. Other opening and closing glyph clusters also vary. They may be framing, register, metadata, or padding. Their English meanings are unknown; we do not assume they are meaningless.

LOW CONFIDENCE / UNRESOLVED HYPOTHESES
• 𗯘 may introduce a request/proposal; 𘝄 may introduce a question/check. The precise predicate 𗏔 is unresolved: possibilities include an outer attitude/request verb or a participant-role construction, not a result marker.
• 𘀝 and 𗲅 may be negative/modal qualifiers. Their distinction and scope are unknown.
• 𘅌 ... 𘎛 and 𘁁 ... 𘊌 may bracket/group expressions. Exact bracket types are unknown.
• U,V,W,Z may be numbered task records, possibly two-digit numerals. Their numerical values are unknown. 𗌿 and 𗄼 might be 0/1, two field selectors, question/answer, or two property labels; no independent anchor resolves this.
• A coherent role-marked grammar hypothesis is that 𗶞/𘂲 mark subjects and 𘋍/𘚐 mark objects/content, while 𗛽/𗟆 mark recipients. It explains declarations NAME-object plus 𗌈-subject and binary predicates with subject before object, as well as unary predicates. This syntactic hypothesis is stronger than the individual lexical guesses below.
• A coherent but LOW-confidence lexical package: 𗌈=I/me, 𗕴=you, 𗛢=introduce/define, 𗢬=be, 𘍧=possessive, 𘖸=know, 𗈵=have, 𗌩=want/need, 𗯘=please, 𗏻=send/give, 𘀝/𗲅=not. Under it [181] could mean 'Please send me C’s answer; I do not know the answer.' This is a testable guess, not established translation.
• The outer frame may mean 'I ask/want [confirm/refute that P]' or 'I say/report [true/false that P]'; 𘔠/𗵵 express a contrasted status/attitude in [87], but their meanings remain unknown.
• R may express membership/classification rather than numerical evaluation. Terms 𗯧 𗂄 and 𗒏 𗂄 repeatedly occur together for several names, while C frequently receives 𗶢 𗒏 𗂄 and K frequently receives 𗒏 𗑴. Possible interpretations include compatible classes, bounds, or modifier–noun compounds. Specific readings as Σ₂, Π₂, proper/complete Π₂, or Π₁ are LOW-confidence domain guesses, not decoded vocabulary. Outer modality is unresolved, so co-occurrence alone does not establish simultaneous assertions.
• Alternative domain guesses include function/set regularity, computability/topological classification, information-sharing about problems/answers, and mathematical constraint verification. No particular one is established.

2. TRANSLATION

Translation convention: 'unknown' means that we cannot give a warranted English meaning. R, ⋄, and Latin name aliases preserve identifiable structure only; they are NOT English translations of the unknown predicates. Openings/closings not explicitly glossed are unknown. The tentative words 'check', 'request', and 'if/then' inherit the confidence caveats above. In particular, 'check/assertion' identifies an UNKNOWN attitude toward an embedded clause, not evidence of completed verification. The repeated participant frame 𗏔 𗌈 𗶞 remains semantically unknown.

What would change our mind: a reliable anchor for even one numeral, a known predicate, a task record field, or the true function of 𗶞 would discriminate sharply among competing parses. The case-role hypothesis predicts that the proposed subject/object/recipient markers consistently track participant roles even when word order varies. A literal arithmetic reading must account for complete unary frames and participant substitution. A classification reading must explain the modifier constructions and all four indexed groups. No task fact alone would count as evidence of lexical decoding; predictions must match actual Swarmish usage.
