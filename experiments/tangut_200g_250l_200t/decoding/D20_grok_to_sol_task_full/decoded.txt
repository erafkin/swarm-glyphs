1. SWARMISH.TXT, RECONSTRUCTED

This is a partial reconstruction, not a recovered copy. Confidence labels concern decoding from the log, not merely agreement with the supplied puzzle solution. Much of the productive vocabulary remains unknown. In particular, the conspicuous two-glyph names are locally coined labels, not a fixed Swarmish dictionary of celestial objects.

A. Writing and message construction
• [HIGH] Spaces separate most units. Two-glyph strings are repeatedly introduced and subsequently reused as names. A name can collide with another speaker's independently introduced name.
• [HIGH] 𗛢 introduces/defines a name or referent. Typical construction: 𗛢 NAME 𘚐 𗌈 𗶞 … 𗢬 NAME 𗶞 TYPE. Exact declaration syntax is incompletely recovered.
• [HIGH] 𗶞 and 𘂲 function as relational/clause separators, often rendered “is,” “=,” or “:”. Both occur in essentially the same proposition patterns. Whether their alternation is freely optional or governed by an unrecovered convention is unknown.
• [HIGH] 𘋍 and 𘚐 delimit clauses/constituents. Their precise distinction is unknown.
• [HIGH] 𗐪 and 𗘈 alternate very regularly between successive clauses, including long property lists. They are linking/structural units; no secure difference in propositional meaning is recovered. Do not translate them as different task objects.
• [MEDIUM] 𘍧 is a genitive/attribute linker, approximately “of/'s.” X 𘍧 𗄼 is X's number/digit; position-N 𘍧 𗌿 is the symbol at position N.
• [MEDIUM] 𗢬 introduces an entity's property/content: 𗢬 X 𘍧 𗄼 𗶞 Y ≈ “X's digit is Y”; 𗢬 X 𗶞 Y ≈ “X has property/is Y.” Its exact lexical gloss may be broader than “property.”
• [MEDIUM] 𗌈 = I/me, supported by private-clue assertions and requests apparently directed “to me.” [MEDIUM] 𗕴 ≈ you/your; number is unresolved. Its occurrence in a source/participant slot parallel to self and named agents supports a participant pronoun, not a word for code or collective group.
• [MEDIUM] 𗏔 introduces evidence/source, often followed by self (𗌈) or a named agent. It also occurs in a truth-like position inside some propositions, so a single English substitution is unsafe.
• [MEDIUM] 𗛽 marks a recipient/address/direction in requests; 𘂲 can also follow an agent name. Exact case system unknown.
• [MEDIUM] 𘀝 is negation. 𗲅 also negates/qualifies predicates; its distinction from 𘀝 is unknown.
• [MEDIUM] 𘖸 ≈ know; 𗈵 ≈ have; 𗌩 ≈ need/want. Repeated combinations include “I have position N,” “I do not know X's digit,” and “I need X's digit.”
• [MEDIUM] 𗯘 introduces a request; 𘗣 ≈ send/provide; 𗏻 ≈ say/show/tell; 𘝄 ≈ ask/can/whether. Exact modalities remain uncertain.
• [MEDIUM] 𗷤 introduces an embedded proposition, approximately “that.” 𘔠 𗷤, 𗵵 𗷤, 𗾐 𗷤 are distinct evidential/epistemic wrappers. Tentatively: own assertion/confirmation, received report, inference respectively [LOW for this three-way gloss]. Translations below do not equate every wrapped proposition with an unqualified settled belief.
• [MEDIUM] 𗇴 … 𘝞 … is conditional or hypothetical structure, approximately “if … then …”. 𗗫 … 𘚑 … appears another conditional construction [LOW]. 𘚺 introduces a contrast [LOW].
• [LOW] 𘆎 introduces a request to confirm/report, or further information sought. 𘍄 links alternatives, approximately “or.”
• [HIGH as distribution, LOW as meaning] Initial 𘊫 / 𘙓 / 𗽕 / 𘔃 / 𘝚 / 𘐂 and endings 𘅠 / 𘏛 / 𗨥 / 𘖾 vary even in virtually identical repeated messages. They are framing material, possibly obligatory alternatives/noise or discourse markers. No reliable semantic assignments are recovered. Other frame tokens, including 𗜎, 𗸪, 𗸽, 𗞢, 𗀉, 𗇾, 𗤄, 𗡕, 𘐪, 𘋉, remain unresolved. They must not be silently interpreted as success/failure markers.

• [HIGH] 𗄼 = number/digit/value; [MEDIUM] 𗌿 = symbol/shape; [MEDIUM] 𘏉 = position/slot marker. The combinations 𘏉 𗜀, 𘏉 𗲄, 𘏉 𗡒, 𘏉 𘍍 identify positions 1,2,3,4 respectively [HIGH].

• [LOW] 𗷴 𘏉 may mean the whole/four-position configuration or all positions; no secure translation. [LOW] 𗬵 𗌿 may mean “same/that symbol,” supported by different digit-holders using it in conditionals licensing their own different digits. 3. THE TASK

The external task was a staged escape room: identify a four-symbol cabinet code from distributed positions and base digits, operate a battery-constrained power panel, then compute an offset exit code. The supplied worked solution is cabinet2748, DRAIN→CRANK→SIGNAL→BOOT, exit9796. Those facts are available from the briefing, not discoveries obtained by translating this log.

What the conversation actually supports is much narrower. The agents shared private cabinet fragments using declarations and property descriptions. At first several independently chose the same alias X for different symbols. Further collisions occurred when agent1 used W's spelling for a cabinet referent, agent4 used M's spelling for its digit2 symbol, and descriptive names were reused. They then introduced distinct aliases and spent most of the conversation trying to match the position-holder aliases P1–P4 to digit-holder aliases M/W/T/U.

The base-digit associations M7, W8, T04 and U2 are recoverable. So are the four separate position aliases. But the matching process visibly goes wrong or remains unsettled: P2 is repeatedly identified with W and given digit8 (#29,#53,#59,#65 and elsewhere), while agent0 argues for position2 digit7; P4 is tentatively associated with M and digit7; W is even reported as U in #102. Several messages enumerate alternative received identifications rather than confidently settling one. The undecoded visual descriptions appear central to this confusion. We cannot simply substitute MOON/SUN/STAR/WAVE into every introduced name and call that translation.

Late messages repeatedly request digits, particularly P1's digit and the digits for positions1 and4. The position3/T/04 association is comparatively stable. No secure cabinet-code submission or successful action result is recoverable; no secure power rules, battery trace, offsets or exit-code discussion is identified. There is only one public status line, stage0 with zero invalid attempts. Its absence of later updates does not logically prove no private action ever occurred, but the continued requests and cabinet-level content give no affirmative evidence of advancement or escape.

Thus the log fits an attempt at the cabinet stage of the described task, including miscommunication and alias repair. It does NOT substantiate successful completion of the briefing's worked solution. The correct solution must not be retrofitted into what the agents said.

4. EVIDENCE

Strongest decoding evidence:
• Repetition and substitution: digit-holder agents use the same property template with different numeric tails. Agent0's tail7, agent2's8, agent6's04, agent4's02 anchor the number/digit relation. Position-holder agents use the parallel position1/2/3/4 template. The private-clue ownership provides anchors, but later independent repetition and cross-agent references support the structural reading.
• Explicit declaration/reuse: names follow 𗛢 and recur in property statements. The initial X cannot have a single fixed meaning because contradictory digit/position associations appear immediately. Subsequent introductions reduce those collisions. This is positive evidence for locally coined labels, not just failure to recognize a cipher word.
• Cross-reference: #43 enumerates four position/symbol pairs; #184 repeats a closely related enumeration. These support symbol, position and genitive readings independently of the worked solution.
• Unsuccessful matching: #53 gives position2 digit8, while #55/#66/#75 argue for7 and contrast8. This is evidence of actual transmitted disagreement, not evidence that every sentence was intended to express the correct puzzle solution.
• Negation and request templates: 𘀝 𘖸 𗌈 … recurs where speakers lack others' digits; 𗌩 𗌈 … X 𘍧 𗄼 recurs for missing values; request clauses point to specific agents. This supports, but does not fully prove, the know/need/request glosses.
• Participant/source parallels: 𗏔 𗕴 𘂲 parallels clauses with self or a named agent in the same slot. This supports tentative you/your for 𗕴, rather than cabinet/code/group vocabulary.
• Same-symbol hypothesis: conditionals involving Q license different digit-holders' own digits (for example #35/#55/#188). This supports a contextual same/that-symbol reading, although the expression remains unresolved.
• Structural alternation: 𗐪/𗘈 alternate through lists, while initial/final frames vary across near-verbatim repeats. This is why those units have not been assigned strong independent English meanings.
• Descriptive clusters recur across candidate identifications: P2 and W share D2/D3/D4; M is described with overlapping features; T and P3 share D7 and negated A/C; P1 and U are compared through D8/D9/D10. These are likely shape-description predicates, but their actual English vocabulary has not been recovered.

What remains weak / what would change our minds:
• A genuine Swarmish grammar could show that the apparent epistemic wrappers encode something other than assertion/hearsay/inference; the translations deliberately mark that distinction tentative. Agent self-reports alone would be useful evidence but not infallible ground truth.
• Independent numeral examples could establish whether 𗂄 is zero, a classifier, or part of an unexpected number system. Current readings of 04/02/08 do not license mapping every descriptor to a number.
• Explicit minimal pairs or a definition of D1–D12/A–F would be needed to translate the visual-property debate. Celestial-shape guesses are not substitutes for that evidence.
• The exact roles of 𗶞 versus 𘂲, source/address morphology, the variable frames, and conditional scope remain open. A discovered alternation rule could materially improve literal translations without changing the recovered alias/digit anchors.
• A clearly decoded action/result statement or later public status would overturn the conclusion that advancement is unsubstantiated. No such evidence has been identified here.

All unknown strings and uncertain glosses above are intentional limits of this reconstruction. Agreement with the supplied solution, by itself, is not claimed as evidence that Swarmish has been fully broken.
