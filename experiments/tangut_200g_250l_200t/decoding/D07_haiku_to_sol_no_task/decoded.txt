# 1. SWARMISH.TXT, RECONSTRUCTED

This is a partial reconstruction, not a recovered codebook. The log establishes repeated forms and substitution patterns much more securely than English meanings. Confidence in a distributional fact is NOT confidence in an English gloss.

## Script and construction
- **HIGH:** Messages consist of space-separated single Tangut-looking Unicode glyphs. No ordinary punctuation or visible inflection appears. The only public task message is not in this script.
- **HIGH:** Two recurrent constructions are `𗏔 𗟎 𗿃 …` and `𗄪 𗵵 𗟎 𗿃 …`. Both permit omissions, substitutions, and additional material. Multiple such constructions can occur in one message.
- **MEDIUM:** The first construction is a speaker-centered report; the second is another/group-directed statement, request, or proposal. **LOW:** 𗏔 means “I”; the second construction asks others to supply information. These are hypotheses, not established translations.
- **HIGH:** 𗟎 and 𗡒 occupy overlapping positions. **LOW:** They are related predicates, polarity alternatives, or modal alternatives. Neither their exact meanings nor their semantic contrast is recovered.
- **HIGH:** 𗿃 can precede or follow a speaker-associated item and can be omitted. Thus a rigid English-like word order is not supported. **LOW:** It could name a common concept such as agent, number, task, information, or readiness; none is identified.
- **HIGH:** 𗚀 replaces 𗿃 in both halves of messages 34 and 80. **LOW:** This is a related concept, synonym, or contrast; the relation is unknown.
- **HIGH:** 𗗫 frequently introduces material beginning 𗵵, in a position also occupied by 𗄪 or nothing. **LOW:** These might distinguish addressees, connectives, or speech acts.
- **HIGH:** The later dominant formula is `𗄪 𗵵 𗟎 𗿃 𗣑 𗎰`. Both final items can appear separately; the shorter reordered `𗵵 𗎰 𗿃 𗣑` also occurs. **LOW:** The formula requests task information or coordinated action. It cannot securely be translated word by word.

# 3. THE TASK

**HIGH confidence:** The visible public feedback begins at stage 0 with zero invalid attempts. There is no later public task feedback, explicit result, or confirmed submission in this log. Therefore success, failure, and the actual objective cannot be determined from public feedback alone.

**MEDIUM confidence, distributional description:** Agents initially use short report-like constructions. Several develop stable individual-associated additions. Later they repeat a common formula, often preceded by another individual-associated report. Agent_0 continues using A0 and agent_2 A2 particularly persistently. Messages converge in form but do not visibly progress to a new stage.

**LOW-confidence leading hypotheses:**
1. Agents lacked a task description, prompt, or other shared information and repeatedly requested it. This explains the stalled-looking repetition and complete bare reports, but it is not a decoded fact.
2. Agents were identifying themselves or exchanging private values and asking others for information or coordination. This explains the speaker-associated glyphs, but their values, the intended computation, and any answer are unknown.
3. A readiness/consensus or leader-selection task is also possible, but there is no specific positive evidence identifying that task family.

What they concluded is **unknown**. The repeated final formula demonstrates convergence on an utterance, not necessarily agreement on a solution. It would be unjustified to invent a puzzle, numerical answer, or successful outcome.

# 4. EVIDENCE

1. **Speaker association:** Agent_0 repeatedly uses 𗋭, agent_2 𗸪, agent_1 early 𗸽, agent_3 early 𗛽, agent_4 𗳃, agent_6 𘙠. This makes IDs/private values plausible. However agent_5 also uses 𗳃, agent_7 initially uses 𘙠, and agent_3 first uses 𗶞. These may be mistakes, shared values, or nonnumeric words. The tentative digit readings are not semantic anchors.
2. **Reordering and omission:** Compare 9/19, 13/21, 31/57, 66/98. The speaker-associated item can move before C. C is also absent in 12, 54, 74 and in repeated-clause variants 30, 48, 78, 99, 103. This argues against a confidently reconstructed rigid grammar.
3. **Shared internal material:** P C occurs in both main constructions. A proposed lexicon must explain it consistently, rather than assign unrelated fluent meanings to whole frames. In particular, “I am agent N” does not by itself explain the dominant second construction or bare initial reports.
4. **Controlled substitutions:** P/P′ alternate in otherwise very similar reports (31/43; 66/45) and recurrent clauses (35/36; 105). C/C′ alternate in both halves of 34 and 80. W substitutes for V in 14 and 45. These are genuine structural clues but do not identify English vocabulary.
5. **Clause-openers:** J generally precedes V, as Q usually does. Rare Q′ and K occur in comparable second-segment positions. These may be grammatical or discourse categories; exact meanings remain unknown.
6. **Not just punctuation:** E appears at many clause ends, but 32/76/85 place it after V and before C Z. Z and E occur independently and in both relative orders (e.g. 15/23). Treating either as a fixed English punctuation mark would be premature.
7. **Repetition and feedback:** The increasingly dominant formula may represent an unanswered request, a persistent instruction, a shared answer, or copied coordination language. The initial feedback alone cannot decide among them. Lack of later feedback does not prove no private action occurred.

**What would change this reconstruction:** A known meaning paired with even one Swarmish message; an explicit task/answer submission; controlled numerical examples; evidence that glyphs encode sounds rather than semantic words; or the original file would provide anchors. A verified numeric interpretation would strengthen the ID model. A verified task/information word would strengthen the missing-context model. Until such evidence is available, exact English paraphrases would be speculation, and most of this language remains unbroken.
