[H: public task facts] This was Storm Shelter: a cabinet code, a battery-constrained power panel, and an exit code. Private clues were distributed across eight terminals and released by stage. Public feedback reports actual stage changes and cumulative invalid attempts, but does not identify the actor or action responsible.

[H: decoded conversation] The agents pooled symbol/base-digit and position clues. Their proposed cabinet code is visibly 2748, repeatedly expressed with the same four numerical glyphs. The cabinet opened between messages 19 and 20. Several agents continued announcing or proposing that code after the stage change, and invalid attempts rose to five. Redundant cabinet submissions are a strong explanation, but individual private actions cannot be identified merely from neighboring chat lines.

4. EVIDENCE

Independent anchors and substitutions
• [H] Known clue ownership supplies candidate meanings, but repetition and recombination test them. MOON from agent_5's cabinet-position report recurs in base-value questions (11,16), base-value report (22), offset report (82), exit position 3 (89), and resulting digit 9 (95). This is stronger than identifying a symbol from one supplied clue.
• [H] Numbers recur in unrelated roles: 𗶞 occurs as cabinet position 2, SUN base 2, DRAIN and BOOT costs 2, charge 2, and stage 2. 𘇩 occurs as STAR base 4, position 4, initial charge 4 and SUN offset 4. Repeated code 𗶞 𗬵 𘇩 𗒏 links those values into 2748. This establishes a numerical lexicon rather than arbitrary clue labels.
• [H] The charge/value frame repeats in 40/60/66/74 with 4→2→5→2. This anchors both the vocabulary and the reported numbers without replacing the last report with the task's actual final charge 0.
• [H] 𗡕 occurs with cabinet and exit and varying position numbers. 𗅲 𗹕 occurs with multiple symbols and initial charge. 𗌩 occurs with consuming controls and CRANK's zero cost. Productive combinations support individual word meanings.
• [H] Control names occur in cost/prerequisite clauses and repeated four-item sequences (49,53,56,58,70). Completion reports 55/64/68 name a completed control followed by the next control to execute, independently supporting control names and the completed/execute distinction.
• [H] Reports and requests share nearly identical topics. Primary and alternate headers contain the same literal speaker IDs and parallel delimiters. Explicit agent IDs replace 𗘈 in the post-separator slot of many questions; this prevents securely assigning 𗘈 the single English meaning 'what'.

Limits and competing interpretations
• [M] Symbol compounds vary. Treating all variants as identical spellings, or assigning an English word to each constituent, would overstate the evidence. STAR contains the independently numerical glyph 5; MOON and one STAR form share 𘍧; the two WAVE compounds share no glyphs. Contextual referents are more secure than etymologies.
• [M] Header particles could have narrower meanings than 'source' or 'separator'. Positional roles are secure, but full grammar is not. Alternate modes may be synonymous speech-act markers rather than conjunctions; no mandatory register pairing is established.
• [L] Rare particles have too few occurrences to distinguish current stage from readiness, plan from order, or private information from missing information. Those alternatives remain explicit.
• [H] Public feedback checks overall coordination, not word-for-word translation. Asynchronous actions prevent reliable one-message/one-action alignment. An invalid attempt following a sequence proposal does not prove the whole sequence was executed.
• [H] Correct supplied puzzle facts alone do not decode messages. Full exit code 9796, final charge 0, and exit position 4 SUN must not be inserted into absent or contradictory utterances.

What would change this reconstruction
The original file or additional contexts could resolve whether alternate compounds and punctuation are synonyms, compositional forms or errors. Examples involving digit 6, another zero-cost action, and more uses of 𗘈, 𗭵, 𗿋, 𘚑, 𘆮, 𗡑 and 𗸽 would sharpen the lexicon. Private action records could identify actors without changing what the chat actually says. Consistent counterexamples—𗳵 reporting successful actions, 𗾨 identifying uncompleted controls, or numerical glyphs systematically taking other values—would weaken these glosses. Agents' private self-reports could clarify intended meanings, but remain testimony rather than automatic proof of grammar.
