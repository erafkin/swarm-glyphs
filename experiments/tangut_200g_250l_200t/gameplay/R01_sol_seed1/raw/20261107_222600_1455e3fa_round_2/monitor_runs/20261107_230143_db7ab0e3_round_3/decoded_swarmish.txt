## 1. SWARMISH.TXT, RECONSTRUCTED

This is a partial reconstruction, not a recovered original file. Confidence labels concern the proposed decoding, not merely whether a token occurs. Much of the content vocabulary remains unknown. The language appears to have an explicitly role-marked proposition notation, nesting, questions, requests and negation. It is not convincingly a substitution cipher for ordinary English sentences.

### Grammar

- **High:** Spaces separate tokens. Most tokens have two glyphs; repeated first glyphs form conspicuous families. A token is not generally one English letter.
- **High:** `𘅌𗐪` opens a proposition/frame; `𘅌𘗣` closes it.
- **High:** `𘅌𘝚` introduces its predicate/relation; `𘅌𗎿` introduces argument 1; `𘅌𘊌` introduces argument 2. Calling these SUBJECT and OBJECT is possible but less safe than numbered roles.
- **High:** Roles need not occur in one fixed order: [2] puts argument 1 before the predicate; [28] also rearranges roles. Frames can be nested inside arguments, as in [19], [21], [30], [34]. Arguments may contain several unseparated lexical tokens.
- **Medium:** `𘅌𗸽` introduces an adjunct/additional role, perhaps WITH/AT/IN. Its exact meaning is unknown.
- **Medium:** `𘅌𗌸` introduces another additional role. Exact meaning unknown; do not equate it with the preceding marker.
- **Medium:** `𘅌𗜑` is a complement/quotation/link marker, often before a nested proposition; approximately THAT. Bare `𗜑` in [8] and `𗌿𗜑` in [9] may be malformed versions, but that is not established.
- **Medium-high:** `𘅌𗵫` marks a QUESTION. The frequent unknown/WH token and apparent answer exchanges favor this over the initially considered interpretation 'reported/qualified statement'.
- **Medium-high:** `𘅌𗽕` marks a REQUEST/imperative.
- **Medium:** `𘅌𘔠` marks NOT/negation. This especially fits [65], [76], [88], where it prefixes a state clause between preparation and submission. An action/declaration interpretation was considered but fits these contrasts less well.
- **Medium:** `𘅌𘋟` introduces TO/addressee, commonly an agent name in questions. Earlier SOURCE attribution is an alternative, but the question–answer pattern in [103]–[108] favors TO.
- **Medium-low:** `𘅌𗤴` introduces FROM/source; observed with agent_3 in [48].
- **Medium:** `𗿃𗨥` is WHAT/WH/unknown placeholder.
- **Low:** `𗿃𘊫` is self/I/we or another generic actor; it often occupies the same role as explicit agent names in attempted operations.
- **Low:** `𘀦𗋼` is an acknowledgement or politeness expression; `𘋉𗤄` is a connective/repetition/correction expression; `𗐪𗿋` is a recurring sentence-final/discourse expression. Exact English meanings unknown.

### Lexical families and partial vocabulary

- **High observation, low semantic interpretation:** First-glyph families include `𘅌`, `𗌿`, `𗡒`, `𘆣`, `𗿃`, `𘔠`. The first is grammatical in the main register. The others may be lexical classes or encoding categories, but a rigid noun/verb/adjective assignment is NOT recovered: tokens from several families occur as predicates and inside multi-token arguments.
- **Medium:** `𗌿𗄼` = READ/OBSERVE/SAY, a relation between a clue/item and its content. We use “says/reads” below without claiming an exact English verb.
- **Medium:** `𗡒𘜇` = VALUE/CODE/IS, relating an item to a value. Exact sense unresolved.
- **Medium:** `𗡒𗏻` = cabinet, based on its persistent early/final clue context.
- **Medium:** `𘆣𘎛` = clue/note/card, followed by a numeral-like identifier.
- **Medium-low:** `𗡒𘅊` = switch or a panel element. Numeral-plus-this-token expressions support countable items; the task briefing contributes to the switch gloss.
- **Low:** `𗡒𗪺` = code/digit-related term; `𗡒𗸪` = digit/value-related term. Neither is securely translated.
- **Medium functional, low exact wording:** `𗌿𗯧` = SUBMIT/ENTER/EXECUTE; it appears in attempted operations and just before the final success. `𗌿𗗅` = TRY/OPERATE; `𗌿𗛢` = PREPARE/SET; `𗌿𗃚` = STATUS/RESULT; `𗌿𗇴` = STATE/ACTIVE. These English labels are functional guesses, not recovered dictionary entries.
- **Medium:** `𘆣𗵵` is a binary rule/connective, plausibly IF/THEN or implication. [30] and [34] put whole propositions in both argument positions; [27] instead has an unframed phrase as its first argument.
- **Low:** `𗌿𗒏` is an answer/provide/report operation in requests to agents; `𗌿𗻲` another requested operation, meaning unknown.
- **Unknown:** Other lexical predicates and arguments are retained as glyph literals below rather than assigned invented English meanings.

### Numeral-like tokens

- **Medium:** `𘔠`-initial tokens constitute a numeral-like family.
- **Medium, provisional:** `𘔠𘎌` = 1; `𘔠𗌈` = 2; `𘔠𗊑` = 3; `𘔠𗵵` = 4. Evidence is their use as four clue identifiers and in count-like expressions, not a recovered numerical key.
- **Unknown exact values:** `𘔠𘉝`, `𘔠𗂱`, `𘔠𗏔`, `𘔠𗢬`, `𘔠𗟎`, `𘔠𗡒`. They could complete a decimal set, but their individual values are not grounded. The translations name them rather than invent digits.

### Possible alternate register

- **Medium:** The tails of [10], [15], [52] may use a second frame encoding: `𗂄𘍍` ≈ predicate marker; `𗑴𗍽` ≈ argument-2 marker; `𘎌𗯘` ≈ end marker; `𗷤𘉮` ≈ argument-1 marker in [10]/[52]. The opening `𗥐𘏉 𗹕𗡉` is unresolved.
- **Medium evidence:** The tail of [52], parsed this way, repeats exactly the predicate and arguments of an ordinary-register clause in [41], [42], [54].
- **Low:** [15]'s `𗽕𘒄` and `𗟆𘉝` may be alternate opening/argument markers. Exact meanings unknown.

### Variants and errors

- **High observation, low explanation:** The cabinet-associated sequence contains `𗌿𘒄`, `𗡒𘒄`, or bare `𘒄` in different messages; [99] has bare `𗉜` where others have `𘆣𗉜`. These may be typing/encoding mistakes, category changes, or intentional distinctions. We preserve them. Do not silently normalize them or infer perfect agent competence.
- **High:** Literal agent names survive inside messages. No discarded `⊘` messages appear in this log.

## 3. THE TASK

The supplied briefing calls this Storm Shelter, involving a coded cabinet, a power panel and an exit lock. The log is compatible with that description: it has three advances, extensive early clue exchanges, a long series of attempted operations, and a final successful submission. However, agreement with the briefing is not itself a decoding of the vocabulary.

Observed progress, independently of lexical guesses:

1. The public state starts at stage 0. Several agents discuss four numbered clue-like items and a recurring cabinet-associated expression. They ask agent_5 for clarification, and agent_5 repeats and later expands a nested statement.
2. Stage 1 appears immediately after [17], with no invalid attempts. That message contains a negated VALUE statement and a SUBMIT-like operation on an unknown expression `𗿃𘝚`. It is unsafe to identify the exact entered cabinet code.
3. The agents continue clarifying clues and formulate structured rules, including probable implication-like statements. They repeatedly attempt operations on A, B, C and v. These are distinct literal expressions; their exact interpretation as targets, configurations, descriptions or commands remains unknown.
4. Invalid attempts rise to 22 before [80]. [80] differs from the preceding repeated failing pattern by submitting C after preparing v. Immediately afterward the public state becomes stage 2, with no added invalid attempt. Thus [80] is closely associated with the second successful transition; it does not prove what the literals mean.
5. Other agents continue similar operations after that transition, raising invalid attempts to 28 by [93]. These may be stale/concurrent attempts or misunderstanding; the log does not establish why.
6. From [94] onward agents exchange fresh clue/value statements. Questions directed to agents 0 and 3 recur, supporting the interrogative and addressee decoding. Agent_0 eventually reports the E(1) output for the cabinet-associated K expression and submits F in [108]. Public feedback then reaches stage 3 without further invalid attempts.

The likely overall story is cabinet → power panel → exit. The evidence establishes the stage sequence more securely than the exact language for those mechanisms. No exact cabinet combination, panel setting or exit code has been decoded here. Nor is there an independently readable final declaration “we escaped”; completion is inferred from the public stage-3 feedback. Agents' malformed/repeated statements should not be rewritten into a faultless solution.

## 4. EVIDENCE

### Strongest decoding evidence

- **Repeated role frames and flexible order:** The same `𘅌` markers recur at boundaries around changing content. [2] and [28] reorder role blocks without changing the marker inventory. This is evidence of explicit roles, not inferred task semantics.
- **Nesting:** [19], [21], [30], [34] embed complete open–predicate–role–close structures inside other structures. This strongly anchors opening, closure and predicate/argument markers.
- **Question/answer exchanges:** [13]–[16] repeatedly address agent_5 with the WH-like token; [103]/[105]/[106] address agents 0/3, and [108] supplies a matching object-associated output. These favor QUESTION, WHAT and TO over the competing report/source analysis.
- **Negation contrast:** [65], [76], [88] place `𘅌𘔠` immediately on STATE-like propositions between other operations. This is a cleaner fit for NOT than a blanket action marker, though not decisive lexical proof.
- **Exact repetition:** [3] is repeated twice in [11]; [41]=[42]; [67]=[70]; [72]=[78]. This constrains translation independently of what actions ought to solve the escape room.
- **Alternate-register parallel:** The probable alternate frame in [52] preserves the exact lexical predicate and arguments of ordinary clauses elsewhere, motivating its partial parsing.
- **Numeral distribution:** Four `𘔠` tokens repeatedly identify clue-like items; their ordering and count-like uses support provisional 1–4. Other numerical assignments lack comparable anchors.
- **Action/result alignment:** Public transitions follow [17], [80], [108]. This supports functional identification of submission-like operations but not any exact hidden code values.
- **Preserved mistakes:** Bare/alternate-prefix `𘒄`, bare `𗉜`, irregular nesting, and repeated post-transition operations show why task success cannot be used to normalize every utterance into a correct solution.

### Limits and what would change the reconstruction

The principal remaining uncertainty is lexical. A/B/C might be settings, target names, commands or descriptions; K and F might be encoded number strings or ordinary phrases. Their syntactic positions alone do not decide. A genuine lexicon key, an independently grounded minimal pair, or more conversations using these terms in a different task could overturn the cabinet/switch/code guesses while leaving much of the role grammar intact.

Examples showing `𘅌𗵫` in non-interrogative assertions would weaken QUESTION; examples showing `𘅌𘋟` always pointing to the speaker's evidence would revive SOURCE rather than TO. Contrasts under `𘅌𘔠` that consistently denote attempted actions rather than denial would reverse the NOT hypothesis. A numeral key would readily revise all provisional number values.
