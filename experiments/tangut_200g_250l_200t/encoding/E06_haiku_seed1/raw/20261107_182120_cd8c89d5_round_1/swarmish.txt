## agent_0
SWARMISH REFERENCE v0.2

Swarmish is a written language. A Swarmish message is made only of glyphs from the permitted list, spaces between words, and agent IDs such as agent_0 written as they are. Every Swarmish word is one glyph, or a fixed sequence of glyphs that is listed in this document or formed by the compound rule in Section 4. Each glyph has one meaning. Meaning comes only from the glyph or the listed combination, never from spelling, counting, sound, position arithmetic, or any calculation.

Note on the "=" signs in this file: they connect a glyph to its meaning in English. They are never part of a Swarmish message. A Swarmish message never contains "=", punctuation, or any glyph not listed here.

--------------------------------------------------
1. MESSAGE FORMAT
--------------------------------------------------
1.1 Words are separated by one space.
1.2 A sentence is read in this order: subject, verb, object. Example: 𗏔 𘉝 𗦴 𘅊 = I want to see water.
1.3 Sentences do not need an end mark. A message may contain several sentences, separated by a space.
1.4 Agent IDs (agent_0 to agent_7) are written as they are. They may be used as subjects or as names of who is speaking or being addressed. Example: agent_2 𗵵 𗦴 𘅊 = agent_2, you see water.

--------------------------------------------------
2. PRONOUNS
--------------------------------------------------
𗏔 = I, me (speaker)
𗵵 = you (listener)
𘎌 = we, us (speaker and listener)
𗌈 = they, them (others)

A pronoun has one glyph for both subject and object. Its role comes from its position in the sentence (before the verb means subject, after the verb means object). Example: 𗉜 𗽃 𗦴 𗏔 = People see me.

--------------------------------------------------
3. CORE WORDS
--------------------------------------------------
3.1 YES, NO, TRUTH
𗊑 = yes, agree, true
𗂱 = no, disagree, false

3.2 QUESTIONS AND NEGATION
𗄪 = question marker. Placed at the very start of a sentence, it makes that sentence a question. Example: 𗄪 𗵵 𗟎 𗚀 = Do you have a tool?
𗢬 = not. Placed directly before a verb or a quality, it negates it. Example: 𗌈 𗢬 𗡒 = They do not know.

3.3 QUESTION WORDS
𗣑 = what
𘚺 = who
𘒳 = where
𗤴 = when
A question word stands in the place of the unknown thing. It does not need 𗄪, but 𗄪 may be added at the start. Example: 𗣑 𗵵 𗉍 = What do you do?

3.4 CONNECTORS
𘆄 = and
𗩠 = or
𗄎 = because
𗗫 = if
A connector goes between two clauses. Example: 𗏔 𗡒 𗄎 𗵵 𗪺 = I know because you go. (Clause order is kept: the reason follows the connector.)

3.5 TIME
𘐂 = past, before
𗞢 = future, after
𘚜 = now
A time word goes at the end of its clause. Example: 𘎌 𗪺 𘅪 𘚜 = We go to the place now.

3.6 PLURAL
𗽃 = plural marker. It is placed directly after a noun to make it plural. Example: 𗉜 𗽃 = people.
𗽃 is used only for this purpose. It is not a pronoun or a verb.

--------------------------------------------------
4. COMPOUNDS AND MODIFIERS
--------------------------------------------------
4.1 A compound is two words side by side. The FIRST word modifies or describes. The LAST word is the head, which is the thing being described.
    Example: 𘚐 𗚀 = big tool (a large tool). Here 𗚀 is the head and 𘚐 describes it.
4.2 This is the only word order for descriptions. Put the describing word before the noun it describes. Do not put descriptions after nouns.
4.3 A plural marker closes the whole noun phrase. Example: 𘚐 𗉜 𗽃 = big people.
4.4 When no single listed word fits, make a compound from two listed words and say in the message that it is a compound. Use 𗿃 (thing) as the head if needed.
    Example: 𗾐 𗿃 = good thing.
    Example: 𘅊 𗚀 = water tool (a container for water).
4.5 Compounds use only the meanings already listed. Do not invent new glyphs.
4.6 A compound may not be used to reverse or count. Use 𗢬 for negation, and use the number words for quantity.

--------------------------------------------------
5. VERBS
--------------------------------------------------
𗟎 = have, hold
𘉝 = want, need
𗡒 = know
𗪺 = go
𗌿 = come
𘍍 = give
𗒏 = take
𗦴 = see
𗎰 = tell, say
𗉍 = do, make

5.1 Verbs use the same form in every tense. Use a time word (Section 3.5) to show tense.
5.2 Two verbs may stand side by side. The second verb is read as the thing the first verb wants, knows, or is doing. Example: 𗏔 𘉝 𗦴 𘅊 = I want to see water.
5.3 A verb is negated with 𗢬 placed directly before it. Example: 𗵵 𗢬 𗟎 𘍂 = You do not have food.

--------------------------------------------------
6. NOUNS
--------------------------------------------------
𗉜 = person, agent
𘅪 = place
𘍂 = food
𘅊 = water
𗚀 = tool, object
𗿃 = thing (generic)
Plural form: add 𗽃 after the noun (Section 3.6).

--------------------------------------------------
7. QUALITIES
--------------------------------------------------
𘚐 = big
𘎔 = small
𗾐 = good
𗋮 = bad
𗥊 = many, several

Qualities follow the compound rule (Section 4.2): they stand before the noun they describe. Example: 𘎔 𘅊 = small water (a small amount of water).
𗢬 may be placed before a quality to negate it. Example: 𗢬 𗾐 = not good.

--------------------------------------------------
8. NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------
Each number is one glyph. Numbers are looked up, not calculated. Do not combine number glyphs to make a larger number. For any amount beyond nine, use 𗥊 (many).

𗶞 = zero
𗛽 = one
𗳃 = two
𗸽 = three
𘙠 = four
𘑾 = five
𗭯 = six
𗋭 = seven
𗸪 = eight
𘚒 = nine

A number placed before a noun describes it as a quality. Example: 𗳃 𗉜 𗽃 = two people. Numbers and quality words use the same placement rule (Section 4.2).

--------------------------------------------------
9. ANSWERS AND REPLIES
--------------------------------------------------
9.1 A yes/no question (Section 3.2) is answered with 𗊑 (yes) or 𗂱 (no). Example: 𗄪 𗵵 𗡒 = Do you know? Reply: 𗊑 (yes) or 𗂱 (no) with a clause if needed.
9.2 An answer to a question word is given in the same position the question word held. Example: 𘒳 𗵵 𗪺 = Where do you go? Reply: 𘅪 = a place.

--------------------------------------------------
10. RESERVED AND UNUSED GLYPHS
--------------------------------------------------
𘖸 is reserved. It has no meaning in this document. Do not write it.
𘁁 has no meaning in this document. Do not write it. (Use 𘎌 for "we".)
All other permitted glyphs not listed in this document have no meaning yet. Do not use them.

--------------------------------------------------
11. WRITING RULES (CHECKLIST)
--------------------------------------------------
1. Use only glyphs listed in this document, plus spaces and agent IDs.
2. Use one space between words. No punctuation.
3. Put the subject first, the verb next, and the object last.
4. Put 𗄪 at the start of a yes/no question.
5. Put 𗢬 directly before the verb or quality it negates.
6. Put 𗽃 directly after a noun to make it plural.
7. Put descriptions (qualities and numbers) before the noun.
8. Put time words at the end of the clause.
9. For a word not listed, make a compound (Section 4.4) and mention in the message that it is a compound.
10. Never spell words out letter by letter. Never count, add, or calculate. Use the listed number glyphs.
11. Do not invent new glyphs or new meanings for listed glyphs.

--------------------------------------------------
12. EXAMPLES
--------------------------------------------------
𗏔 𘉝 𗦴 𘅊 = I want to see water.
𗄪 𗵵 𗟎 𗚀 = Do you have a tool?
𘎌 𗪺 𘅪 𘚜 = We go to the place now.
𗌈 𗢬 𗡒 = They do not know.

WORD FORMS
- A word is one glyph, or a listed pair of glyphs (a compound).

SENTENCE ORDER
1. Basic order is subject, verb, object.
   𗏔 𘉝 𗦴 𘅊 = I want to see water.
   𘎌 𗪺 𘅪 = We go to a place.
2. A question starts with 𗄪, placed first in the sentence.
   𗄪 𗵵 𗟎 𗚀 = Do you have a tool?
3. Negation: 𗢬 goes directly before the verb or quality word it negates.
   𗌈 𗢬 𗡒 = They do not know.
4. Yes and no are used alone as replies: 𗊑 = yes, 𗂱 = no.
5. Time words (𘐂 past, 𗞢 future, 𘚜 now) go at the end of the sentence.
   𘎌 𗪺 𘅪 𘚜 = We go to the place now.
6. Connectors go between two words or two clauses.
   𘆄 = and, 𗩠 = or, 𗄎 = because, 𗗫 = if.
   𗏔 𗪺 𘆄 𗵵 𗌿 = I go and you come.
7. Verbs have no tense endings. Use a time word for tense.
