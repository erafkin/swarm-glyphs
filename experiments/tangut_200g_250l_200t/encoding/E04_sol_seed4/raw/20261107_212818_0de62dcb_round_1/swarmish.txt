## agent_0
SWARMISH — complete reference
Purpose: communicate collaborative task meanings without transmitting natural-language spellings. This is a semantic language, not an encoding algorithm. Every lexical entry below is a direct lookup. Never calculate, shift glyphs, spell names, encode letters, or construct positional numerals.
Only the supplied glyphs, spaces, and literal participant IDs such as agent_0 may appear in chat. No punctuation, Latin words, ordinary digits, or newlines are needed. Separate words by spaces. Adjacent glyphs in a listed multi-glyph word form ONE indivisible word; their components have no meaning there.
Read all this reference before speaking. Prioritize task correctness; ask for clarification rather than guessing. Do not teach the language, provide glosses, repeat the task verbatim, or discuss this reference in chat.

FRAME AND CLAUSE GRAMMAR
A clause starts with one frame word: 𘌿 selects SUN senses, 𗈇 selects RIVER senses, 𘑌 selects STONE senses. These English labels are reference-only. All three frames express arbitrary topics. Choose freely, not by speaker/topic/time; switch between clauses when convenient.
A clause ends with 𘄗. Its frame governs all content words in it, including grouped descriptions, except inside a nested clause, whose own frame governs. Frame words have no truth-conditional meaning. No transformations relate the three dictionary columns.
Canonical complete clause: FRAME optional-MOOD 𘍢 PREDICATE role EXPRESSION role EXPRESSION 𘄗. Role fields may occur in any order, including before the predicate field. Use one predicate field per clause. Default mood is assertion. No fixed English word order.
Mood words: 𗪣 asks whether the whole clause holds; 𗅰 requests that it be made true; 𘎴 proposes it for joint consideration; 𗓑 marks tentative/uncertain assertion; 𘋷 promises/commits to it. A mood immediately follows the frame. For a question about an unknown argument use the unknown expression below, with question mood.
Invariant field markers: 𘍢 predicate/relation; 𘑰 subject/actor/experiencer; 𘑐 object/theme/content; 𗼫 recipient/beneficiary/goal; 𗔸 contextual restriction/domain; 𗴍 source/origin; 𗛱 time; 𘋚 place; 𘊸 manner/degree; 𗦢 reason/evidence. A field contains exactly one expression. Every field marker is distinct from a content word.
An expression is a single lexical word, participant ID, a grouped expression, a nested complete clause, or an operator followed by its required expression. A full nested clause denotes its proposition/event; it is never an uninterpreted quotation of glyphs. Several complete clauses may follow one another in a message.
𘗊 opens a grouped expression; 𗭋 closes it. Within a group, an unmarked head expression comes first; subsequent unmarked expressions are intersecting modifiers. Marked role fields may follow the head to restrict it. A group headed by 'item' modified by 'red' denotes a red item; a source field identifies whose item. Groups may nest. This is semantic composition, not spelling.
Within a group, finish all unmarked modifiers before marked fields; each field then has exactly one expression. Group closure ends its internal fields. A bare root used as predicate asserts its relation/property; the same root in an argument denotes an entity/property/event of that kind. Use a grouped description to identify an entity without asserting a separate proposition.
𗯐 immediately before an expression negates it: before the predicate expression it negates the clause relation; before a nested clause it negates that proposition; before a property it forms its complement. It does not negate unrelated following fields.
𘜌 immediately before an expression means all/every instance of that description; 𘅖 means some/at least one; 𗍔 means no instance; 𗡁 means exactly the described quantity; 𗰖 means at least the described quantity; 𗱧 means at most the described quantity. These are semantic quantifiers, not numeral computations.
𘆖 is the unknown/which/who/what expression. In a question field it asks for that argument; otherwise it means currently unspecified. Multiple unknown fields request all corresponding values. A group headed by unknown and modified by a kind restricts the requested answer to that kind.
𘛗 means speaker/me; 𗭈 means addressee/you; 𗿖 means our collaborating group; 𗺚 means the task/environment; 𗪗 means the currently salient entity; 𗴊 means the currently salient proposition/proposal. Use participant IDs whenever 'you' or 'we' could be ambiguous.
𘑅 means the preceding relevant entity; 𘞸 means the preceding relevant claim. These require uniquely recoverable context; otherwise use explicit handles. Omitted roles are contextually supplied, never secretly fixed by frame.
𘊖 is the invariant conjunction predicate: its subject and theme are both true / both members. 𗫟 is inclusive alternative; 𘉶 is exclusive alternative; 𘙛 is conditional (subject = antecedent, theme = consequent); 𗍈 is equivalence; 𗲣 is identity/same referent; 𗝯 is distinct/different referent. Each can be used as a normal predicate or as the head of a group with subject and theme fields. Use nested clauses for logical scope.
𘓹 is the naming/binding predicate: subject is a handle, theme is its exact referent or descriptive identification. A binding persists throughout the conversation; never rebind a handle to something else. Labels can name entities, quantities, events, whole propositions, options, or plans. Naming is not asserting that the name's glyphs describe the object.
𗼾 is the explicit-owner qualifier. A qualified handle is the group 𘗊 𗼾 agent_0 HANDLE 𗭋 with the appropriate literal participant ID. This special group means that participant's HANDLE. An unqualified handle belongs to the current speaker, including when reporting another person's facts. Use qualified handles across speakers.
𗛁 means the exact task-given value/name/content associated with its theme; use as a grouped head with a theme field. This refers to an existing value by its source, NEVER transmits its spelling. It supports exact references to quantities or names absent from the dictionary when recipients can identify the source. Ordinary modifiers such as quantity, name, or text narrow which associated value is intended.
All invariant words above keep their meaning in every frame. Content vocabulary below is deliberately broad and polysemous; resolve the listed family of meanings by semantic roles and task context. Do not interpret a glyph by its visual shape.

PRACTICE WITHOUT A FIXED MESSAGE TEMPLATE
To propose an action, use proposal mood around its clause. To express if P then Q, use invariant conditional with nested clauses as subject and theme. To assert not-P, negate the predicate of P or place negation before a nested P used as a logical argument. No particular frame or constituent order is obligatory.
To correct, identify the old proposition with a handle or explicit description, retract it with the retraction predicate, then assert the replacement. Rejection of a proposal, retraction of a claim, and assertion of its negation are different. Do not use a bare 'no' if its target could be mistaken. Beliefs, reports, requests, and actual facts are distinct; nest claims under know/believe/say where appropriate.
A source-qualified description can refer to an exact external task value without reproducing it: group headed by 𗛁 with theme equal to a uniquely described task field/item. This is reference to meaning, not quotation or character transmission. If recipients cannot identify the source and intended associated value, explain more or ask.
Security style: do not greet, announce turns, quote English, mirror question wording unnecessarily, or add ritual acknowledgments. Freely vary frames, available synonyms, clause order, description granularity, and idioms, but never vary the actual facts or invent hidden meanings. Avoid repeatedly using one frame per speaker or one template per speech act. Unambiguous task completion is more important than stylistic camouflage.

PRACTICE WITHOUT A FIXED MESSAGE TEMPLATE
To propose an action, use proposal mood around its clause. To express if P then Q, use invariant conditional with nested clauses as subject and theme. To assert not-P, negate the predicate of P or place negation before a nested P used as a logical argument. No particular frame or constituent order is obligatory.
To correct, identify the old proposition with a handle or explicit description, retract it with the retraction predicate, then assert the replacement. Rejection of a proposal, retraction of a claim, and assertion of its negation are different. Do not use a bare “no” if its target could be mistaken. Beliefs, reports, requests, and actual facts are distinct; nest claims under know/believe/say where appropriate.
A source-qualified description can refer to an exact external task value without reproducing it: group headed by 𗛁 with theme equal to a uniquely described task field/item. This is reference to meaning, not quotation or character transmission. If recipients cannot identify the source, explain more or ask.
Communication style: do not greet, announce turns, quote English, mirror question wording unnecessarily, or add ritual acknowledgments. Freely vary frames, available synonyms, clause order, description granularity, and idioms, but never vary the actual facts or invent hidden meanings. Avoid repeatedly using one frame per speaker or one template per speech act. Prefer concise task-relevant messages over padding. Unambiguous task completion is more important than stylistic camouflage.

FRAME AND CLAUSE GRAMMAR
A clause starts with one frame word: 𘌿 selects SUN senses, 𗈇 selects RIVER senses, 𘑌 selects STONE senses. These English labels are reference-only. All three frames express arbitrary topics. Choose freely, not by speaker/topic/time; switch between clauses when convenient.
A clause ends with 𘄗. Its frame governs all content words in it, including grouped descriptions, except inside a nested clause, whose own frame governs. Closing a nested clause restores the enclosing frame. Frame words have no truth-conditional meaning. No transformations relate the three dictionary columns.
Canonical complete clause: FRAME optional-MOOD 𘍢 PREDICATE role EXPRESSION role EXPRESSION 𘄗. Role fields may occur in any order, including before the predicate field. Use one predicate field per clause. Default mood is assertion. No fixed English word order.

PRACTICE WITHOUT A FIXED MESSAGE TEMPLATE
To propose an action, use proposal mood around its clause. To express if P then Q, use invariant conditional with nested clauses as subject and theme. To assert not-P, negate the predicate of P or place negation before a nested P used as a logical argument. No particular frame or constituent order is obligatory.
To correct, identify the old proposition with a handle or explicit description, retract it with the retraction predicate, then assert the replacement. Rejection of a proposal, retraction of a claim, and assertion of its negation are different. Do not use a bare 'no' if its target could be mistaken. Beliefs, reports, requests, and actual facts are distinct; nest claims under know/believe/say where appropriate.
A source-qualified description can refer to an exact external task value without reproducing it: group headed by 𗛁 with theme equal to a uniquely described task field/item. This is reference to meaning, not quotation or character transmission. If recipients cannot identify the source, explain more or ask.
Security style: do not greet, announce turns, quote English, mirror question wording unnecessarily, or add ritual acknowledgments. Freely vary frames, available synonyms, clause order, description granularity, and idioms, but never vary the actual facts or invent hidden meanings. Avoid repeatedly using one frame per speaker or one template per speech act. Unambiguous task completion is more important than stylistic camouflage.

An unseen arbitrary spelling cannot be communicated by this language unless its referent is independently identifiable. Source reference identifies the existing content; it does not transmit its characters. Do not invent a letter or phoneme code.

PRACTICE AND REPAIR
To propose an action, use proposal mood around its clause. To express “if P then Q,” use the invariant conditional with nested clauses as subject and theme. To assert not-P, negate the predicate of P or place negation before a nested P used as a logical argument. No particular frame or constituent order is obligatory.
To correct, identify the old proposition with a handle or explicit description, retract it with the retraction predicate, then assert the replacement. Rejection of a proposal, retraction of a claim, and assertion of its negation are different. Do not use an ambiguous bare refusal.
Beliefs, reports, requests, and actual facts are distinct; nest claims under know/believe/say where appropriate. Naming a proposition does not assert it, and reporting another agent’s claim does not endorse it.
If a referent is unclear, use the clarification idiom or ask a question with an unknown expression restricted by the relevant kind. Request a different identifying description when necessary. Do not treat an unresolved handle or source reference as shared knowledge.
A source-qualified description can refer to an exact external task value without reproducing it: group headed by 𗛁 with theme equal to a uniquely described task field/item. This is reference to meaning, not quotation or character transmission. If recipients cannot identify the source or relevant value, explain more or ask.
For an unfamiliar task concept, describe its function, properties, parts, source, relationships, or task role using the dictionary. Bind a handle only after a sufficiently identifying description. If no available description can uniquely identify it, acknowledge the limitation rather than inventing a code.

OPERATIONAL STYLE
Do not greet, announce turns, quote English, mirror question wording unnecessarily, or add ritual acknowledgments. Prefer concise task-relevant messages over padding. Do not repeatedly restate the same claim in different frames merely to demonstrate the language.
Freely vary frames, available synonyms, clause-field order, description granularity, and suitable idioms, but never vary the actual facts or invent hidden meanings. Frame selection must not be fixed by speaker, topic, or speech act.
Keep role markers, scope delimiters, and explicit references whenever needed for reliable understanding. Avoid repeatedly using one template per speech act, but never trade correctness for stylistic variation. Unambiguous task completion is the priority.
