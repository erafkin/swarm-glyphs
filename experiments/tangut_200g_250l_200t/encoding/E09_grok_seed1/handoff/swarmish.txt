SWARMISH
A lookup language. Meaning is only what this file assigns. Do not spell English or any other language. Do not use glyph shape, inventory order, or any count or calculation. No digits except inside an agent ID.

ALLOWED
Spaces, agent IDs agent_0 agent_1 agent_2 agent_3 agent_4 agent_5 agent_6 agent_7, and the glyphs listed below. Anything else is forbidden. Each inventory glyph below is assigned once. 𗶢 is the same word in both mentions. Examples reuse those assignments.

TOKENS
Split on spaces. Each token is one listed glyph, an agent ID, or a name of exactly two clay glyphs with no space. A lone clay glyph is an error. A two-glyph token must be two clay glyphs and is one name, never two words. Look up every other glyph alone. If two notes clash, the LEXICON block wins.
A slash joins alternate single glyphs of one word. Alternate them across your own messages. Do not treat the pair as one token or as two meanings. There is no separate grammar block; the lists in this file are the lookup.

MESSAGE SHAPE
1. First token: one opener. It means nothing. Vary it.
   Openers: 𘊫 𘙓 𘝚 𗽕 𘔃 𘐂
2. Last token: one closer. It means nothing. Vary it.
   Closers: 𘅠 𗨥 𘏛 𘖾
3. Dust glyphs mean nothing. Skip every dust token wherever it stands. Do not delete glyphs inside a clay name.
   Dust: 𗜎 𗇾 𗄪 𗀉 𗤄 𘍠 𗸪 𘂮 𗸽 𘋉 𗞢
   𗎶 is only the digit 9. It is not dust.
   Writers put one, two, or three dust tokens after the opener and before the closer. Never first or last. Never after a question glyph. Not required inside a quote.
4. After the opener: one or more clauses. A question glyph, if any, is the token immediately before the closer. It marks the whole message as a question.
5. Yes/no reply: opener, yes or no, dust, closer. Nothing else is required.

CLAUSE
Order inside a clause: optional now, then the verb group, then role phrases in any order.
Optional topic, before now and the verb group: HEAD topic. The topic is what the clause is about. It has no case. Topic is only this slot, never a separate message slot.
  topic: 𘇩
There is no mood word. Question is only the glyph immediately before the closer.
Linkers are not inside the verb group.
  if and then stand immediately before the clause they mark. if marks the condition; then marks the result. Pattern: if CLAUSE then CLAUSE.
  and, or, but, and because stand between two clauses, after the first clause's last phrase. because means the following clause is the cause. Pattern: CLAUSE because CLAUSE.
Two phrases with the same case in one clause are joint in that role.

VERB GROUP
Order: (not) (please) (modal) VERB (tense).
not scopes over the verb of that group, not over please, a modal, or later phrases: 𘀝 / 𗲅
please: 𗯘 / 𘆎
modal, at most one: maybe 𗡉 ; must 𗈽 ; can 𗋟 ; want-to 𗡑
want-to is for an action. The verb want is for a thing.
very, optional, immediately before the verb: 𗶢
past, after the verb: 𗭯
future, after the verb: 𘆄
No tense glyph means present or timeless.
After the verb group and before its role phrases, verbs say, know, think, ask, want, confirm, deny, and explain may take one quoted clause as their content. That quote is what is said or known. It is not a relative and not a separate main claim. Quotes do not nest. A quote holds one clause and no linker.
A content quote is only a quote whose open glyph is the first token after the verb group. A quote later, after a head, is a relative.
With be, the agent phrase is the subject and the patient phrase is what that subject is.

PHRASES
Role comes only from the case glyph, never from order.
Shape: (this|that) (modifiers) HEAD (with HEAD) (plural) CASE
HEAD is a lexicon noun, pronoun, question word, agent ID, coined name, or a bare this/that.
Modifiers are lexicon words before the head. only is not a modifier.
this: 𘉮
that: 𗉍
plural, just before case: 𘎛
also, just after the phrase it focuses: 𗜑
only, just after the phrase it restricts: 𗽃
with joins two heads that share one case: 𗎿
Cases:
  agent (doer) 𗶞 / 𘂲
  patient (done-to) 𘚐 / 𘋍
  recipient 𗛽 / 𗟆
  location (at or to) 𘁁 / 𗨞
  source (from) 𗘝
  time 𘊌 / 𗱑
  manner 𗍽
Possession is a phrase: OWNER poss OWNED, then modifiers of the owned, then one case for the whole phrase.
  poss: 𘍧
A relative clause sits after the head and before case. Wrap it in quotes. The head is the missing argument of that clause. Quotes on a head do not make a new claim.
  quote open: 𗷤
  quote close: 𗏔
A clause may be a verb plus one phrase when the speaker is only pointing at that phrase.
Digit words, if any, follow the head and precede case. They are a label in written order, not a sum.

SMALL PATTERNS
go plus a location phrase: motion to that place.
come plus a source phrase: motion from that place.
take plus a source phrase: motion or transfer from that place.
be plus a location phrase: at that place.
this plus time case, no noun: now. that plus time case: at that time. next plus time case: later.
this plus location case, no noun: here. that plus location case: there.
whole before a head: all of it. other before a head: another. false is only the modifier false, never none.
now may also stand alone immediately before the verb group as a time adverb.
A run of digit words inside one phrase, before its case, is a label in written order. It is not a place-value instruction and not a sum. Read one two as the label one-then-two, not as twelve.
Colors, tools, directions, and comparison are coined names, usually beside color, thing, place, or way.

HOW TO READ
Drop the opener token, the closer token, and every dust token. Split on spaces. Find each verb group. If a quote open is the first token after that verb group, it is content. Attach following phrases by case until and, or, but, or because, or until if or then that begins the next clause. if and then stand immediately before the clause they mark; and, or, but, and because stand between clauses. Look up every single glyph in LEXICON or the lists above. Treat a two-clay token as a name introduced with name. Agent IDs point at teammates. A question glyph before the closer asks about the whole message. If a message is still unclear, ask in Swarmish.
